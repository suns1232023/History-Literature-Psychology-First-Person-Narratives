# The Self-Made Emperor: Emperor Xuan of Han

### *The Twenty-Sixth Day of the Seventh Month, Fourth Year of Diji — Weiyang Palace, Rain*

The register lies face down on the table.

Ink has seeped through the paper, like blood, but not quite.

Blood, once completely dry, turns brown.

This ink is freshly ground—black as night, still carrying the faint smell of pine soot.

The names circled in vermilion are packed tightly together, one upon another.

Like a game of chess already finished.

Every black piece dead.

Every white piece dead.

**Nothing left on the board but emptiness.**

I should have smiled.

But I did not.

I tried to move the corners of my mouth.

The muscles obeyed.

But it was not a smile.

**It was a movement I had practiced for seven years. The muscles remembered. My heart did not.**

Ji Ying's shadow lay across the threshold, stretched long by the candlelight until it reached my feet.

Thirty-five years ago, there had been the same shadow in the prison cell of the Palace Service.

A starving infant, too weak even to cry.

That shadow standing between him and the palace servants.

“He is still only a child.”

Tonight, that shadow refused to cross the threshold.

I ordered the servants to close the door.

The sound was soft.

Like a bone slipping back into place.

Then the entire hall belonged only to me.

Me.

The register.

The candle.

And the smell of pine soot rising from the ink.

I turned the register face down.

But the ink had already seeped through.

**The names rose from the other side of the page like corpses floating beneath dark water.**

---

That leaking-roof house in Duxian.

Pingjun catching the rain in a basin.

*Ding. Ding. Ding.*

She curled up against me and said,

“It sounds like someone playing the qin.”

I was twenty-three then.

I had nothing.

And yet I thought I could live that way forever.

Then Huo Guang came.

---

The day we went to worship at the Grand Ancestral Temple, he rode beside me in the imperial carriage.

He sat on my right.

The carriage was small.

His shoulder touched mine.

There were two layers of clothing between us.

But it felt as though I had been pressed against a red-hot iron.

He sat perfectly upright, immovable as a mountain.

I shrank toward the other side, every muscle rigid, cold sweat soaking my back.

**That was when the splinter went in.**

It lodged somewhere between the vertebrae.

Bending hurt.

Straightening hurt.

Lying down hurt even more.

It followed me when I ate.

When I went to court.

When I slept.

And in the spaces between dreams, it would suddenly drive itself deeper, waking me from shallow sleep.

---

### Spring, Third Year of Yuanshi

She was propped against the headboard.

Her face was so pale it seemed almost transparent.

Her eyes were still curved like crescents.

“Bingyi, I had a dream today.

I dreamed of our old house in Duxian.

The roof was leaking.

You were catching the rain in a basin.

I told you that one day you would become a great official.

And you said you didn't want to be a great official.

You only wanted to farm the land with me.”

She smiled.

Barely.

Then she asked:

**“Bingyi, do you still want to farm?”**

I said,

“Yes.”

She said nothing more.

She simply looked at me.

For a very long time.

Three days later, she was gone.

Huo Xian had ordered Chunyu Yan to poison her medicine.

As she died, her hand slowly grew cold.

Little by little.

Like an autumn river freezing over.

**I held that hand from warmth to coolness, from coolness to ice. The temperature was carved into the lines of my palm. It would never leave me.**

Huo Guang came to apologize.

He stood outside the palace doors, in exactly the same place where Ji Ying's shadow stood tonight.

He said,

“My wife acted without restraint. I shall punish her accordingly.”

I walked to the doorway.

I smiled at him.

“It has nothing to do with the Grand General.”

Then I returned to the hall.

And overturned the entire imperial desk.

Ink splattered across the wall like black flowers suddenly blooming.

I crouched down.

One piece at a time.

I picked everything up.

Wiped it clean.

And put it back exactly where it had been.

---

### Autumn, Second Year of Diji

### Beside Huo Guang's Deathbed

I held his hand.

It was cold.

The knuckles were large and hard, like an old weapon worn smooth by years of use.

He looked at me for a long time.

His eyes had grown cloudy.

But his gaze still had the grip of iron tongs.

“You hate me.”

“I would not dare.”

He smiled.

He rarely smiled.

“Of course you dare.

You simply don't dare let me see it yet.”

He coughed for a while.

When it stopped, he said:

**“Liu Xun, you think you are different from me. But sit in that chair long enough, and you will become me. It isn't a question of whether you want to. That chair turns everyone who sits in it into the same kind of man.”**

I said nothing.

---

After Huo Guang died,

I waited three years.

I warmed the water slowly.

Pingjun had taught me how.

One winter in Duxian, she had been boiling beans.

She told me the fire could not be too fierce.

If it was too hot, the beans would burst.

You had to simmer them slowly.

Xu Fu submitted memorials.

Zhang Chang urged action.

Wei Xiang remonstrated.

I told them all,

“I know.”

But I did nothing.

I waited.

They rebelled.

Just as I knew they would.

Troops surrounded the Huo residence.

In a single day,

**the entire clan was executed.**

That day I stood upon the city wall.

The flames consuming the Huo family illuminated half of Chang'an.

The wind carried smoke into my face.

It made my eyes water.

The people beside me said:

“His Majesty is overwhelmed with grief, remembering the Huo family's former service.”

Only I knew what was inside that smoke.

**Seven years of hatred.**

**The coldness of Pingjun's ashes.**

**Those mornings in the imperial carriage when sweat soaked through my inner robes.**

**That splinter lodged between my vertebrae.**

At last,

everything burned to ash.

**But what does ash taste like?**

**Bitter.**

---

At today's court, Wei Xiang reported that the Xiongnu were showing signs of renewed activity.

There were too few commanders.

The frontier garrisons were dangerously thin.

I had killed dozens of generals associated with the Huo family.

Overnight,

half the northern defenses of the Han Empire had become empty.

When Huo Guang was alive, the Xiongnu did not dare look south.

He sat in that chair with power in his heart.

But at least he knew where to use it.

**I sit in this chair with hatred in my heart.**

**I turned the military power of an entire empire into a private weapon for avenging Pingjun.**

“You will become me.”

Those three words suddenly rose from the deepest part of my memory.

I had not summoned them.

But they came anyway.

Like something that had lain for years at the bottom of a well,

slowly rising on its own.

I did not catch them.

I let them sink again.

---

The rain gradually stopped.

A sliver of moonlight appeared through a break in the clouds.

Pale.

Almost bloodless.

It fell upon the blue-gray bricks before the palace.

I stood.

Put on my imperial robes.

Fastened the jade belt.

The bronze basin beside me reflected my face.

My temples had turned white.

My eyes were still bright.

I smiled at my reflection.

Precise.

Gentle.

Perfectly controlled.

Then I lifted the basin and poured the water onto the ground.

The reflection shattered.

The water disappeared into the cracks between the bricks,

mingling with the rain.

I walked out of the sleeping chamber.

The sky was gray-blue.

The clouds hung low.

Inside the Xuan Room, the ministers saw me enter.

They fell to their knees as one.

“Long live the Emperor.”

I stood upon the imperial steps.

And began to speak.

My voice was strong.

Every word perfectly formed.

Outside,

the rain began again.

It struck the glazed roof tiles.

*Ding. Ding. Ding.*

Like someone playing the qin.

I heard Pingjun's voice:

**“Do you still want to farm?”**

I heard Huo Guang:

**“You will become me.”**

**I did not answer.**

I said:

**“Rise, gentlemen.”**

Then I began to listen to Wei Xiang's report on the northern frontier.

Approved.

Rejected.

To be reconsidered.

One word after another.

Steady.

Clear.

Irrevocable.

**The sound of rain passed through the palace walls, crossed seven hundred li of dust and earth, and fell upon Duling.**

**I did not look back.**

**…**
