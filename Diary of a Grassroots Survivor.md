# **Diary of a Grassroots Survivor**

### *A Neurobiological Journey of a Man Who Never Stopped Looking for a Stronger Patron*

---

## **Year 198 AD · The Night Before Xiapi Falls**

### **The 15th Day of the Third Month · Overcast**

Tonight, outside Xiapi, the campfires of Cao Cao's army spread across the darkness like countless stars. Their flickering light comes through the bars of my prison cell at White Gate Tower, alternately illuminating and swallowing the room.

The ropes around my wrists bite into my flesh.

The pain reminds me of many years ago, when I first gripped the reins of a horse on the grasslands of Bingzhou. Back then, I thought that if only I could ride the fastest horse, I could outrun every pursuit, every enemy, every fate that hunted me.

Looking back now, I realize that I have spent my entire life doing the same thing:

**Searching for a stronger patron.**

From Ding Yuan to Dong Zhuo, from Dong Zhuo to Wang Yun, then to Yuan Shu, Yuan Shao, Zhang Yang, Liu Bei... I was like a carp leaping from rock to rock in a raging river. Every time I jumped, I thought I had found a safer place to land.

I never realized that every landing was carrying me deeper into the whirlpool.

Sometimes I wonder:

Why couldn't I stop?

Those sons of great families will never understand. They were born sitting on the strongest legs in the world. Yuan Shao came from four generations of three Excellencies. Cao Cao's father had been a Grand Commandant. They never needed to search for protection.

Protection came looking for them.

But what about me?

I was Lu Bu.

My father died when I was young. My mother was gone by the time I was eight. I grew up like wild grass on the grasslands of Bingzhou, and every day I asked myself the same question:

**Will I still be alive tomorrow?**

Survival.

That was the instinct carved into my bones.

I still remember the afternoon I first saw Red Hare.

It was a wild horse that even the stablemen were afraid to approach. Its eyes carried the untamed ferocity of the northern plains.

I walked toward it.

Everyone thought it would kick me to death.

Instead, I mounted it.

And it became mine.

At that moment, something exploded inside my head.

Years later, a physician from the Western Regions told me that the sensation had a name:

**dopamine.**

He said it was part of the brain's reward system, a signal that tells us:

*You did something right. Do it again.*

From then on, I became addicted.

Every time I found a stronger patron, my brain seemed to explode into fireworks.

There was that feeling when Dong Zhuo gave me Red Hare.

There was that feeling when he promoted me.

There was that feeling when he gave me gold and women.

But the pleasure always came quickly—and disappeared just as quickly.

Before long, I needed a greater stimulus to hear the fireworks again.

**The death of Ding Yuan was the first great explosion of my life.**

To be honest, I barely hesitated when I killed him.

Not because I hated him.

Because I calculated.

How strong was Ding Yuan's protection?

How strong was Dong Zhuo's?

When the shadow of the latter swallowed the former, my brain made its decision.

It rewarded **upgrading**.

It punished **loyalty**.

People who condemn me as a "three-surnamed slave" will never understand.

In their civilized world, loyalty is a virtue.

But on the grasslands of Bingzhou, **loyalty is often the first thing the wolves eat.**

---

## **The 16th Day of the Third Month · Rain**

I have been tied up all day again.

The ropes cut into my flesh. I could not sleep.

I stared at the spider webs hanging from the ceiling and thought about Dong Zhuo.

He was the strongest patron I had ever embraced.

When Dong Zhuo entered the capital, how many troops did he bring?

Three thousand?

Five thousand?

No.

Far more.

His Xiliang cavalry thundered across the stone streets of Luoyang, and the entire court trembled.

I stood behind Ding Yuan and watched that fat man ride through the city gate.

I remember thinking only one thing:

**If I stand beside him, who will dare to kill me?**

Eventually, I did stand beside him.

The day he accepted me as his adopted son, I knelt before him and bowed my head to the ground.

My eyes filled with tears.

Not because I was moved.

Because, for the first time in my life, I believed I was safe.

Who in the world had a stronger leg than Dong Zhuo?

He was Chancellor.

Then Grand Tutor.

He held the sword.

A sword that could cut down anyone.

But surely it would never cut down his own son.

I did not yet understand the truth:

**The stronger the leg, the harder it hurts when it kicks you.**

When Dong Zhuo lost his temper, he would throw his hand halberd at me.

It was iron.

When it struck my shoulder, pain shot through my body.

Yet I did not dare move.

Once, the halberd passed less than two inches from my ear and buried itself in a wooden pillar.

It vibrated there, humming.

I knelt on the ground, lowered my head, and smelled the metallic scent of blood.

And I thought:

**No matter how strong this leg is, it still belongs to someone else.**

How far was I from death that day?

**Less than two inches.**

From that day onward, I carried an invisible ruler inside my mind.

I used it to measure every person.

The distance between me and him.

The strength of his desire to kill me.

How long I might remain alive beside him.

I called it the **killing line**.

The killing line is not a physical distance.

It is time.

It is the knowledge that you do not know when you will die—but you are certain that you eventually will.

It is like having a blade pressed against your throat.

You do not know when it will fall.

But every second, you can feel its cold edge.

That night, I sat alone in my tent, replaying the scene again and again.

The sound of the blade cutting through the air echoed in my mind throughout the night.

I touched my neck.

That invisible line.

Less than two inches away.

And that was when I learned my second lesson:

**Embracing a powerful patron is not the real objective.**

**The real skill is surviving after you have embraced one.**

It took me ten years to understand that.

---

## **The 17th Day of the Third Month · Wind**

During the days of Wang Yun's conspiracy, it felt as though my mind had been split in two.

One half was Diaochan.

The other half was Dong Zhuo.

One half wanted the woman.

The other half wanted to live.

My brain became a battlefield.

Two armies fought inside my skull, advancing and retreating day after day.

In the Kunqu opera *The Chain Stratagem*, the scene is portrayed vividly: the famous **three advances and three retreats**.

I would embrace Diaochan, then push her away.

I wanted to kill Dong Zhuo, then lost my nerve.

People laughed and called me cowardly before women and timid before men.

They did not know what was happening inside my brain.

The physician from the Western Regions once told me that such conflict could be understood through the language of modern neuroscience:

**the conflict between the prefrontal cortex and the limbic system.**

One voice said:

*She is beautiful. I want her.*

Another said:

*Dong Zhuo will kill you. Run.*

Those two voices fought inside me for an entire month.

I could barely sleep.

I could barely think.

Then I killed Dong Zhuo.

When the blade came down, the greatest fireworks of my life exploded inside my head.

Dopamine seemed to flood through me like a broken dam.

My entire body trembled.

Not from fear.

From exhilaration.

For the first time, I was my own master.

But the excitement lasted less than two months.

Dong Zhuo's former followers returned.

Li Jue.

Guo Si.

The two Xiliang commanders I had once looked down upon came roaring into Chang'an with Dong Zhuo's surviving forces.

My army was too small.

I could not hold the city.

So I ran again.

The night I fled Chang'an, the wind was fierce.

Red Hare raced through the darkness.

I leaned low over his back and looked once over my shoulder at the city.

The gates were closed.

Torches burned everywhere.

I knew I would never return.

What I did not know was that from that night onward, **I would never stop running.**

---

## **The 18th Day of the Third Month · Clearing Clouds**

After killing Dong Zhuo, I entered the wildest period of my life.

I went to Yuan Shu.

He rejected me.

He said I was "unpredictable."

He said I was difficult to control.

I waited outside his tent for three days and three nights.

Finally, a servant came out and said:

"My lord says you should seek another patron."

I went to Yuan Shao.

He accepted me.

Then he tried to kill me.

I helped him win a great battle, yet that very night he sent men to steal my horses.

I pretended to sleep inside my tent.

I heard footsteps outside.

My hand remained on the hilt of my sword.

I did not close my eyes all night.

At dawn, I mounted Red Hare and rode away.

I went to Zhang Yang.

But Zhang Yang himself was struggling to survive.

He feared Cao Cao.

He feared me.

I was a burden he wanted desperately to throw away.

Eventually, I went to Liu Bei.

Liu Bei was different from all the others.

When he received me, he did not look me over as though weighing my usefulness.

He simply said:

"General Lu has nowhere else to go. Stay here for the time being."

Something moved inside me.

Just once.

Then I took Xuzhou from him.

People say that was betrayal.

But do you understand?

**When a man has spent his entire life being used as someone else's weapon, and suddenly finds himself holding a weapon of his own, he does not easily give it back.**

It is not necessarily because he does not want to.

It is because he is afraid.

He does not know whether he will survive after returning it.

**Xuzhou was mine.**

For a time, those years were among the most peaceful of my life.

But my mind still would not quiet down.

My followers began to drift away.

I stopped listening to Chen Gong's advice.

I failed to appreciate Gao Shun's loyalty.

I became increasingly irritable.

Increasingly stubborn.

Increasingly unable to hear anyone but myself.

I knew something was wrong.

But I could not control it.

The physician from the Western Regions once told me that prolonged exposure to power and reward could alter the way a person responds to feedback and regulates emotion.

At the time, I did not understand.

Now I wonder if he was talking about me.

My reward system kept whispering:

**Find another stronger patron.**

**Find another stronger patron.**

**Find another stronger patron.**

---

## **The 19th Day of the Third Month · Overcast**

Today, my own men betrayed me.

Hou Cheng.

Song Xian.

Wei Xu.

They bound me.

They bound Chen Gong as well.

Then they delivered us to Cao Cao.

Cao Cao sat inside his tent and looked down at me.

His eyes were different from Dong Zhuo's.

Different from Yuan Shao's.

Different from everyone else's.

They were the eyes of a hunter looking at prey.

Not anger.

Not contempt.

Just calm examination.

As though he were inspecting a captured weapon and deciding where to place it.

He asked:

"Lu Bu, will you surrender?"

I knelt on the ground.

And suddenly, my mind became quiet.

For ten years, my mind had never been this quiet.

The fireworks disappeared.

The dopamine disappeared.

The killing line disappeared.

For the first time, I experienced something I had almost forgotten:

clarity.

I looked at Cao Cao.

And I thought:

**Is this the final and strongest leg?**

He was perhaps the strongest patron in the world.

He held the Emperor.

He commanded armies.

He had brilliant advisers.

If even Cao Cao could not protect me, perhaps no one could.

I was about to speak.

Then Liu Bei said:

"Have you forgotten how Lu Bu served Ding Jianyang and Dong Taishi?"

At that moment, the killing line snapped into place around my throat.

And I finally understood.

**The problem was never that the patron was not strong enough.**

**The strategy of constantly searching for a stronger patron had a ceiling.**

You can always search for a stronger protector.

But the killing line moves closer every time you betray someone.

Change one patron, and it moves one inch.

Change ten, and it is already against your throat.

**Even the strongest leg cannot defeat the killing line.**

The killing line was never outside me.

**It had always been inside my own choices.**

The physician from the Western Regions might have described it in terms of an over-sensitive stress-response system.

My early life on the grasslands had taught my brain to remain alert to danger.

Even when I was safe, I kept scanning the horizon.

Always looking for threats.

Always preparing to run.

Always searching for the next escape route.

But now I was safe.

Because I would never have another chance to run.

---

## **The 20th Day of the Third Month · Dawn**

Dawn is coming.

Cao Cao's men will soon arrive.

In the final moments of my life, I finally understand something.

If I could live again, I would not search for a stronger patron.

I would do four things.

### **First: I would rebuild my ability to trust.**

The physician once told me about oxytocin and social bonding.

Perhaps my early life had taught me to treat relationships as transactions rather than attachments.

I wanted closeness.

But I could not trust anyone.

If I could live again, I would try to trust one person.

Not use him.

Not depend on him.

Truly trust him.

Even if only once.

### **Second: I would learn to remain calm in the presence of danger.**

My stress-response system was always on high alert.

The smallest disturbance could send my body into fight-or-flight.

That made me fast.

It made me dangerous.

But it also meant I could never truly enjoy safety.

If I could live again, I would learn to breathe.

I would learn to tell myself:

Not every sound in the darkness is a death sentence.

### **Third: I would make my reward system depend on inner achievement rather than external attachment.**

I spent my life chasing external validation.

Higher office.

Greater power.

A stronger patron.

A larger army.

A greater name.

Every achievement became the beginning of the next craving.

If I could live again, I would learn to find satisfaction in riding horses, practicing archery, reading books.

I would learn to look at the scenery beside the road instead of staring only at the killing line ahead.

### **Fourth: I would learn self-compassion.**

I never loved myself.

I was merely a tool.

A weapon.

A blade.

A stone rolling through a violent age.

I spent my entire life trying to please others, trying to remain useful, trying to ensure that someone would keep me.

But I never asked myself:

**What do I actually want?**

If I could live again, I would be kinder to myself.

I would tell myself:

You deserve to be treated well.

Not because you are useful.

Not because you are powerful.

But because you are you.

But it is too late.

---

# **Epilogue: The Revelation at White Gate Tower**

The rope is already around my neck.

Dawn has broken.

Sunlight enters through the window and falls gently across my face.

Warm.

For a moment, I close my eyes.

I remember the wind of Jiuyuan.

I remember Red Hare.

I remember the terrified faces of the men watching me perform my famous feat at the gate.

At that moment, I was a weapon.

But even a weapon has dignity.

Those who call me "three-surnamed slave" never spent a single day surviving the winds of Jiuyuan.

They never slept with a blade beside them.

They never woke in the night wondering whether the killing line had moved one inch closer.

They write about me safely from their scholarly chambers.

They do not know.

I, Lu Bu, killed countless men.

I served countless masters.

I died beneath White Gate Tower.

But when I died, I did not kneel.

**I spent my entire life trying to survive.**

And in the end, I discovered that survival itself was not the highest purpose.

Some things matter more than staying alive.

The killing line had always been inside me.

And the only way to break it was not to find a stronger patron.

**It was to become strong enough to stand without one.**

Not to embrace someone else's leg.

But to become the leg others might one day seek.

I understood this too late.

If you are a grassroots survivor like me—

If you are always searching for a stronger patron—

If you live in fear of your own killing line—

Then stop for a moment.

Think.

**You do not need a stronger leg.**

**You need a heart that is no longer afraid.**

The rope tightens.

The wind stops.

Everything ends.

---

# **Postscript: The Ghost of Lu Bu**

### **Qingming Festival · Year 2,223 After the Founding of Jian'an**

I do not know how long I have been dead.

My spirit has wandered through the world.

I have watched dynasties rise and fall.

I have watched countless heroes appear.

I once thought a man like me was born only once in a thousand years.

Then I discovered something.

Every age has its Lu Bu.

They do not bear my name.

But I recognize their eyes immediately.

They are always looking for a stronger patron.

Always calculating the killing line.

Recently, I saw someone.

His name was **Huang Guochang**.

He was born more than two thousand years after me, on an island far to the east.

I heard that he came from an ordinary background.

He studied his way upward, became a professor, and eventually entered an institution called the "Legislative Yuan."

He was restless.

Like me.

When I looked at him, it felt strangely like looking into a mirror.

---

## **The First Mirror: The Escalation Game of Finding a Stronger Patron**

My life moved from Ding Yuan to Dong Zhuo, from Dong Zhuo to Wang Yun, then to Yuan Shu, Yuan Shao, Zhang Yang, Liu Bei...

Every time I switched sides, I believed the next patron would be stronger.

Huang Guochang seemed to be walking a similar road.

Early on, he was associated with the **Democratic Progressive Party**, which accommodated his candidacy in the 2016 legislative election.

Later, he joined the **New Power Party** and eventually became its chairman.

That was like killing Dong Zhuo and believing I had finally become my own master.

But perhaps I was still someone else's weapon.

Later, when the New Power Party became weaker, he left.

Like me fleeing Chang'an.

He moved again.

He eventually joined the **Taiwan People's Party**.

There, he became a legislator through the party-list system.

Then, after Ko Wen-je became embroiled in legal and political controversy, Huang assumed the chairmanship of the party.

From grassroots politics to party leadership.

In less than a decade.

From one political organization to another.

From one alliance to another.

I watched with fascination.

I had once moved from subordinate officer to cavalry commander, from general to Marquis, from dependent warrior to ruler of Xuzhou.

The heights can be reached quickly.

But remember:

**The higher you climb, the harder the fall.**

---

## **The Second Mirror: The Extreme Logic of Risk Avoidance**

I also saw reports concerning Huang Guochang's family and overseas arrangements.

It made me pause.

I, Lu Bu, never thought that way.

I knew only how to mount Red Hare and flee.

He seemed to understand something I never did:

**risk can be distributed.**

A person can create an escape route before the crisis arrives.

From the perspective of modern psychology, however, there is a strange paradox here.

The more external safeguards a person constructs, the more attention the mind may devote to the possibility of danger.

Safety measures can protect us.

But they can also remind us that danger exists.

I did the same thing in my own time.

I built walls.

I surrounded myself with guards.

I tried to eliminate every possible threat.

Yet I never felt safe.

The more protection I accumulated, the more threats I imagined.

I wondered whether modern people experience the same paradox.

Does security always produce security?

Or can the endless pursuit of security become another form of fear?

---

## **The Third Mirror: In the Eyes of the "Adoptive Father," I Was Always a Weapon**

This is the realization that hurts me most.

Ding Yuan used me because I could ride and fight.

Dong Zhuo used me because I could kill and intimidate.

Wang Yun used me because I could kill Dong Zhuo.

Yuan Shu, Yuan Shao, Zhang Yang, Liu Bei—

each had reasons to use me.

But who stayed because of **Lu Bu himself**?

No one.

**I was a weapon.**

When the weapon was useful, everyone wanted it.

When a sharper weapon appeared, the old one could be discarded.

I was a tool in Ding Yuan's eyes.

A tool in Dong Zhuo's eyes.

A tool in Wang Yun's eyes.

And eventually, a tool in the eyes of everyone who needed me.

Looking at modern politics, I wondered whether political actors sometimes encounter the same dilemma.

A party may value someone because that person can mobilize voters.

Another organization may value the same person because he can attack opponents.

An alliance may value him because he can win elections.

But strategic usefulness is not the same thing as personal trust.

**Being useful is not the same as being trusted.**

That was the mistake I never understood.

---

## **The Fourth Mirror: The Killing Line Is Always Inside You**

This was the lesson I understood only before my death.

I believed that if I found a stronger patron, I would move farther away from danger.

So I kept changing sides.

But I never understood:

**The killing line is not attached to the patron.**

**It is attached to your history of choices.**

Every betrayal changes how others evaluate your future behavior.

Every alliance switch creates a new calculation:

*Will he stay?*

*Will he leave?*

*Will he eventually betray us as well?*

That is why trust is so difficult to rebuild.

I remember the day Hou Cheng, Song Xian, and Wei Xu bound me.

Why did they act first?

Perhaps because they no longer believed I would remain loyal to them.

They saw the killing line.

And they decided to move before I did.

I do not know what will happen to anyone in the future.

But I know this:

When trust disappears, fear takes its place.

And once fear becomes the foundation of a relationship, the relationship becomes unstable.

---

# **The Final Revelation: Even the Strongest Leg Cannot Save You**

Before I died, Cao Cao was perhaps the strongest patron in the world.

I looked at him and thought he could save me.

Then Liu Bei spoke.

Cao Cao withdrew his protection.

Why?

Because Cao Cao understood something I did not.

He knew that a person who had killed Ding Yuan and Dong Zhuo might one day turn against him as well.

**Even the strongest patron has a killing line of his own.**

And so I wondered about modern politics.

What happens when an alliance is built primarily on strategic necessity?

What happens when a more useful ally appears?

What happens when the balance of power changes?

The problem is not simply that one can change patrons too many times.

The deeper problem is that every transition leaves information behind.

Your history becomes part of your bargaining position.

Your reputation becomes part of your power.

Your previous choices become part of every future negotiation.

Eventually, you may discover that the strongest patron in the world cannot erase your past.

---

# **A Message to the "Modern Lu Bu"**

If you could hear the voice of a ghost who died two thousand years ago, I would tell you this:

**Stop searching.**

There is no patron powerful enough to guarantee your safety forever.

There is no alliance that can permanently eliminate uncertainty.

There is no position high enough to make fear disappear.

And there is no amount of external power that can substitute indefinitely for internal security.

You may believe that you are constantly upgrading.

Perhaps you are.

But perhaps you are also accumulating something else:

**a history that others will remember.**

You think you are moving toward safety.

But every move has a cost.

Every broken relationship changes the next relationship.

Every broken promise changes the value of the next promise.

Every strategic defection changes the probability that others will trust you again.

The true way out is not to find a stronger patron.

**It is to become strong enough not to need one.**

Build trust.

Do not consume it.

Stop.

And ask yourself:

**What am I actually afraid of?**

I was afraid my entire life.

In the end, I discovered that I was not truly afraid of Dong Zhuo.

Not Cao Cao.

Not any enemy outside me.

I was afraid of myself.

I was afraid I was not strong enough.

Not safe enough.

Not useful enough.

I was afraid that if I became useless, no one would want me.

But now I understand.

**The greatest security is not having the strongest patron.**

**It is no longer needing one.**

I could not do it.

Could you?

The rope tightened around my neck.

This time, it was not White Gate Tower two thousand years ago.

It was the eternal White Gate Tower.

Where is yours?

I do not know.

And I hope you never find it.

The wind rises again.

The wind from Jiuyuan.

It has been blowing for two thousand years.

And it still has not stopped.
