# **The Red Boy’s Diary: Ashes of the Flaming Mountains**

### *— To My Little Sister, Whom I Never Truly Understood*

> **A Note:**
> Since returning from the South Seas, I have often dreamed of the Ashen Terrace at the Flaming Mountains. In my dreams, Pingping is still the little, fluffy fox who stumbles after me, calling, *“Brother, wait for me.”*
>
> I always wake to find my pillow damp.
>
> I know now that some words, if left unspoken for too long, will eventually become words no one can ever hear.

**Keywords:** Mythology · Family · Trauma · Memory · Redemption · Chinese Mythic Fiction

---

## **The South Seas, Purple Bamboo Grove**

### **The Quiet Chamber of the Good Wealth Boy**

**Seventh day of the Seventh Month, Year of Guimao**

The tide breaks against the shore.

Its sound has not changed in a thousand years.

I open my hand.

The fire jujube lies in my palm, shriveled and withered, no larger than a child's fist. Beneath the glaze of the crystal lamp, its skin has turned an ancient reddish brown—

like dried blood,

like the stain left by tears,

like everything that should have been forgotten, yet somehow refuses to disappear.

Pingping.

Today is Qixi again.

If you were still here, you would be learning how to thread a needle. What would they teach you to embroider?

Flames?

Clouds and thunder?

Or perhaps a crooked little fox clutching a jujube and smiling foolishly?

I wonder sometimes:

If you were to push open this door now, look up at me, and call—

*“Brother.”*

Would I kneel and beg your forgiveness?

Or would I continue pretending to be the Holy Infant King, the creature who died long ago?

---

# **Volume I — The First Meeting**

### *The Flaming Mountains, Year Unknown*

## **I**

The first time I saw you was in the shadows of the Cloud-Covered Cave on Mount Jilei.

You were curled inside Father's arm.

Back then, I still called him *Father*.

Your fur was still crimson, soft and unfinished, your tail no longer than my little finger. The Jade-Faced Princess sat beside the stone couch, dipping her fingertip into warm milk and feeding you one drop at a time.

Outside the cave lay a world of blazing fire.

Inside, the air was strangely cool.

I would later learn that this was the natural chill of the fox clan of Qingqiu, a coldness like moonlight hardened into frost.

I hid behind a stalactite and watched you through the entire night.

The next day.

And the day after that.

And ten days later.

Your fur slowly faded toward silver. A pale golden mark appeared between your brows. They called it the *Moon Scar*, the mark of the royal bloodline of Qingqiu.

On the night of your first full moon, you opened your eyes.

They were the color of glazed glass.

They turned slowly in the darkness, passing over the luminous minerals in the cave, over the exhausted face of the Jade-Faced Princess—

and finally stopped exactly where I was hiding.

You smiled.

You did not even have teeth yet.

But you smiled.

And that single smile made me believe in reincarnation for the next three hundred years.

I convinced myself that you must have known me—

in some forgotten age,

in some universe already swallowed by time.

---

## **II**

The third word you ever spoke was *“Brother.”*

I was outside practicing with my spear.

My control over the Samadhi True Fire was still poor. I had burned half my sleeve when you came stumbling through the doorway.

You fell down three stone steps.

You did not cry.

You simply climbed back up and kept coming.

Behind you, the Jade-Faced Princess called out in alarm. Father laughed and shook his head.

You reached my legs, wrapped your tiny hands around my robe, and looked up.

“Brother.”

You dragged out the final syllable, your voice still carrying the faint sweetness of milk.

From that day onward, you became my shadow.

When I went to the fire spring in the rear mountains, you rode on my back and counted clouds.

When I went to gather fire jujubes from the cliffs, you sat beside the basket and stole them one by one.

When my mother beat me for losing control of the flames, you clung to my knees and cried until the Iron Fan Princess finally turned away in anger.

At night, you crawled into my bed.

Your little hand touched the blisters on my palm.

“Does it hurt?”

“No.”

“You're lying.”

You leaned closer and blew gently across my skin.

“Mother says blowing on it makes it better.”

And somehow—

it did.

Only then did I learn that even frost and moonlight can soothe a wound burned by fire.

---

## **III**

Father sometimes took us out riding.

He would reveal his true form—

a colossal blue-furred bull, his back broad as a moving mountain.

I would sit at his neck.

You would sit in my arms.

Your tiny hands clutched my robe.

Below us, the ravines of the Flaming Mountains plunged into darkness. Rivers of subterranean fire twisted through the depths like red serpents.

You were frightened and buried your face against my chest.

But every few moments, you peeked through your fingers.

“Brother, can the fire reach us?”

“No. Father can breathe out a cloud and make it rain.”

“But Father is a cow.”

“A bull.”

“Bulls can use magic?”

“Yes.”

“What can Brother do?”

“I can breathe fire.”

“Show me!”

So I opened my mouth and released a tiny flame.

It blossomed in the wind like a lotus.

You clapped your hands.

The golden-red light danced inside your glass-colored eyes.

It seemed as though the entire Flaming Mountains had been captured inside them.

Those days were good.

So good that I believed the word *Brother* meant one thing:

that I would always stand between you and the world,

shielding you from every fire,

every storm,

every cold wind that might hurt you.

I did not yet understand how fragile *forever* was.

# **Volume II — The Fracture**

### *The First Years of the Holy Infant King*

## **IV**

How long does forever last?

The answer began to break apart the day I learned the meaning of two words:

**illegitimate son.**

Mother—the Iron Fan Princess—had never once gone to Mount Jilei.

One day I asked why she would not visit you.

She was polishing the Iron Fan.

Her hand stopped.

“That is not your sister.”

Her face appeared in the bronze mirror.

Cold.

Sharp.

Almost like a blade.

Soon I began hearing whispers among the mountain spirits.

*Concubine.*

*Mistress.*

*Fox woman.*

*Illegitimate child.*

I did not understand all of them.

But I understood the poison in people's eyes.

The Jade-Faced Princess smiled less and less.

She would often sit with you at the edge of the cliffs, staring into the sea of clouds as though waiting for something that would never come.

One evening, I could not find you.

I searched through the caves and finally heard muffled sobbing from deep inside.

The Jade-Faced Princess was crouched in the shadows.

Her shoulders shook violently.

You were holding onto her leg.

Your face was tilted upward.

You said nothing.

You simply cried.

Tears fell from your eyes onto the stone floor, one after another, making dark little flowers.

I walked over and lifted you into my arms.

You immediately wrapped yourself around my neck.

Your tears soaked through my clothes.

The warmth made my heart tighten.

That night, I took you outside to look at the stars.

The sky above the Flaming Mountains usually glowed red from the eternal fires below.

But that night it was unusually clear.

The Milky Way stretched across the darkness like a river of milk about to overflow.

“Brother…”

Your voice was muffled against my chest.

“Why was Mother crying?”

“It's an adult matter. Don't ask.”

“She said she only has me.”

You were silent for a long time.

Then you whispered:

“Big Mother has Brother, doesn't she?”

“…Yes.”

“Then that's good.”

You curled closer.

“Brother, you have to stay on my side. Always.”

I did not answer.

I only held you tighter.

Starlight fell through the branches and settled on your silver fur, outlining you with a fragile glow.

You looked so delicate.

As if one careless touch might shatter you.

That was the moment I first hated the fact that I was only a demon and not a god.

If I had been a god, perhaps I could have stopped the stars.

Perhaps I could have frozen that night forever.

---

## **V**

But fractures always reach the surface.

The day I left Mount Jilei to establish my own domain on Mount Hao, you followed me for thirty miles.

You were so small.

You stumbled over the mountain path.

One of your embroidered shoes fell somewhere behind us.

Your bare foot was cut by the rocks.

You did not stop.

You only cried:

“Brother!”

“Don't go!”

“Brother!”

I pulled the reins of the fire cloud and looked back.

You had fallen.

You got up.

You fell again.

Finally, you collapsed into the dust.

Your face was covered in tears and dirt.

Morning sunlight broke through the clouds and stretched your shadow across the road.

It was impossibly long.

Like a wound that would never heal.

“I'll come back,” I said hoarsely.

“When?”

“Soon.”

“Promise?”

You raised your little finger.

There was still dirt underneath your fingernail.

I bent down.

My finger was covered with scars from the fire.

I hooked it around yours.

“Pinky promise.”

“A hundred years.”

“A hundred years.”

For demons, a century is hardly more than a breath.

But we believed it was forever.

We believed promises could survive time.

We were children.

We were wrong.

---

# **Volume III — Two Different Roads**

### *The Beginning of the Good Wealth Boy*

## **VI**

Eventually, I became the Holy Infant King.

My name spread across the eight hundred miles of Mount Hao.

Then Guanyin captured me and sealed me within the Jade Vase.

I was taken to the South Seas.

I became the Good Wealth Boy.

The dew in the Purple Bamboo Grove tastes bitter.

The sound of the tide never stops.

Day after day, it sounds like thousands of dead voices asking the same question.

I began spending long hours sitting beside the lotus pond.

The surface reflected the sky.

And sometimes, in that reflection, I saw your face.

You must have grown into a young woman by then.

Did you still smile?

Did you still believe the lie I had told you?

*I'll be back soon.*

Five hundred years passed.

At last, I was permitted to return.

You were waiting outside the Cloud-Covered Cave beneath an old pine.

You wore a lotus-colored dress.

A silver sash wrapped around your waist.

A simple jade hairpin held your hair.

You looked almost like a mortal girl.

When my cloud descended, you froze.

For a moment, you simply stared at me.

Then you lifted your skirt and ran.

Barefoot.

Across the moss.

Like a bird finally returning to its nest.

“Brother!”

You threw yourself into my arms.

The force nearly knocked me backward.

I caught you instinctively.

Your shoulders were thin as a cicada's wing.

But they were trembling violently.

Then your tears soaked through my robe.

That warmth was painfully familiar.

It was the same warmth as the tears you had shed on my shoulder centuries ago.

“I thought…”

You could barely speak.

“I thought I would never see you again.”

I stroked your hair.

It now reached your waist.

My throat tightened.

Pingping.

My little sister.

How could I tell you?

The one who had returned was no longer your brother.

He was an empty shell eaten hollow by vengeance.

A heart preserved in the salt of ambition.

But all I could say was:

“I came back.”

Sunlight filtered through the pine branches and scattered across your face.

You looked up and smiled.

There were still tears in the corners of your eyes.

But the smile was as pure as it had been when you were a child.

As though the centuries of separation had only been a shallow dream.

The dream was over.

And your brother was still here.

---

# **Volume IV — The Killing**

### *The Final Record of the Holy Infant King*

## **VII**

But every dream must eventually end.

I learned the truth of my birth.

I was the surviving heir of the Yaksha royal house.

The only survivor of the massacre at Spirit Mountain.

I learned that the Bull Demon King—no longer my father—possessed the Great Sage's Sacred Relic.

It was the key to my revenge.

He refused to give it to me.

So I decided to take it.

I asked you to meet me at the Ashen Terrace.

I told you we were going to play the game we used to play as children.

“You run.”

“I'll chase you.”

“One stick of incense.”

That was the rule.

You transformed into your white fox form and leapt into the dead forest.

Before disappearing among the trees, you looked back at me.

Your eyes were filled with absolute trust.

With happiness.

For one instant, I almost shouted:

*Stop.*

But my tongue was already carrying the mantra of the “Great Cause.”

I pursued you slowly.

My Fire-Tipped Spear shattered the rocks where you hid.

The Samadhi True Fire swallowed the dead branches beneath your feet.

Smoke filled the forest.

I saw you running through it.

Your silver-white fur was covered in ash.

Like moonlight buried beneath dust.

Within half an incense stick, I had driven you to the edge of the cliff.

“Brother, that's cheating!”

You were breathing hard, but you were laughing.

“You used real fire!”

I helped you onto the stone platform beside the cliff.

Then I performed a few movements with the spear.

You clapped.

You always loved watching me practice.

Then you reached into your sleeve and pulled out a little paper packet.

Inside were two bright-red fire jujubes.

“I saved these for you.”

You handed me one.

You bit into the other.

“The old tree in the back mountain finally bore fruit.”

Then you began telling me about a little bird whose nest had been blown down in a storm.

You had rescued it.

I interrupted you.

“Pingping.”

“This world has no true peace.”

“There is only the peace people call self-deception after they realize they cannot win.”

You stopped.

The fire jujube slipped from your mouth.

It rolled across the stone.

“Brother?”

Your voice trembled.

“What are you talking about?”

I wanted to tell you not to ask.

I wanted to tell you to run.

But you reached for my sleeve.

Your eyes were still as pure as untouched snow.

How could eyes like that understand hatred?

How could they understand the poison forged by vengeance?

So I raised my hand.

And struck you unconscious.

My palm touched the back of your neck.

Your skin was warm.

Soft.

Still carrying the memory of the little girl who once called me *Brother*.

I carried you into a stone chamber I had prepared beforehand.

I sealed the entrance with a barrier.

Before leaving, I looked back.

You were curled on a bed of dry grass.

Your silver hair spread around you like moonlight.

A trace of red from the fire jujube remained on your lips.

You looked asleep.

As though you might wake at any moment,

rub your eyes,

and call:

*Brother.*

---

# **Epilogue — The Fire Jujube**

### *The Final Words of the Good Wealth Boy*

## **VIII**

Everything that followed became legend.

Some said you woke, broke through the barrier, fell from the cliff, and disappeared without a trace.

Some said the former followers of the Jade-Faced Princess rescued you and erased your memories. You became an embroiderer in some distant town.

Others said you became a strand of moonlight, wandering the Ashen Terrace every night, waiting for a brother who would never return.

I do not know which story is true.

I know only this:

When I killed the Bull Demon King,

when I took the Sacred Relic,

when I unleashed the fire that turned heaven and earth red—

the fire jujube in my robe suddenly became scorching hot.

I opened my hand.

It was shriveling.

Wrinkling.

Drying.

Until it became what it is now.

A hard, ugly fruit.

A bitter fruit that can never again be swallowed.

Pingping.

If I could return to that moment on the Ashen Terrace—

to the moment when you held out the fire jujube,

your eyes bright with trust,

and asked:

“Is it sweet?”

I would say:

“Yes.”

Then I would swallow everything I had never dared to say.

Every secret.

Every poisonous plan.

Every cruelty disguised as the “Great Cause.”

I would choose to be nothing more than that foolish brother who breathed little flames just to make his sister laugh.

But time never turns backward.

Just as a fire jujube cannot return to its branch once it has fallen,

a person who has walked a road paved with blood can never completely wash the stains from his feet.

The tide rises again.

This time, I hear it clearly.

Among the thousands of voices, one word keeps returning.

“Brother.”

“Brother.”

“Brother.”

---

*Inside a rosewood box, beneath a fold of silk, lies a single shriveled fire jujube.*

*Beside it rests a folded sheet of paper.*

*The final lines have been blurred by water.*

*The ink has dissolved into small, indistinct shadows—*

*perhaps tufts of fur,*

*perhaps tears,*

*perhaps the ashes of something that once burned.*

## **Author's Note**

This work is a piece of **mythological psychological fiction**.

It draws upon *Journey to the West* and related narrative traditions while incorporating elements associated with the contemporary game *Black Myth: Wukong*, particularly the character of Pingping.

The psychological portrayal of the Red Boy and his sister is deliberately literary rather than historical or canonical. It employs concepts from analytical psychology, family-systems theory, trauma studies, identity formation, moral injury, memory studies, and narrative psychology to reconstruct a relationship that is largely absent from the classical text.

The purpose is not to establish a “hidden truth” about the characters.

Rather, it is to ask a different question:

**What happens when a child who once existed only to protect his little sister becomes an adult whose pursuit of revenge demands that he destroy everything he once loved?**

The Red Boy's tragedy is therefore not simply that he lost his sister.

It is that he eventually became the very person from whom he once wanted to protect her.

And perhaps that is why the fire jujube remains in his palm.

Not as evidence of what happened—

but as evidence of what could have been.

A reminder that sometimes the most devastating tragedy is not death.

It is the moment when someone who once loved you becomes unable to recognize you as someone worth saving.
