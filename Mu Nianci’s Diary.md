# **Mu Nianci’s Diary**

Someone once asked me:

**“Do you know who wrote you into being like this?”**

I shook my head.

All I know is that my name is Mu Nianci.

*Nianci*—to long for one’s mother.

My adoptive father gave me that name because he missed Bao Xiruo, my mother in all but blood. That is the only thing I know for certain.

And yet everything I have suffered is hidden inside those two characters.

*Nianci, Nianci.*

A lifetime spent longing.

Longing for a mother I would never touch.

Longing for Yang Kang, who would never turn back.

Only now, after all these years, am I beginning to understand who the man was who wrote that name down.

---

## **Volume I · The Name**

I did not know what my adoptive father was thinking when he named me.

Only later did I understand.

He was thinking of Bao Xiruo—his beloved, Yang Kang’s mother, the woman taken away by Wanyan Honglie and separated from him forever.

He put all his longing for her into those two characters.

*Nianci. Nianci.*

In the old language, a father was *jia yan*, the stern one of the household; a mother was *jia ci*, the merciful one.

To long for *ci* was to long for one’s mother.

Perhaps my adoptive father was crying when he named me.

I will never know. I was too young.

But my whole life was decided inside those two characters.

I longed for mercy.

I longed for a mother.

I longed for Yang Kang.

**A name is a road. Once you are placed upon it, you walk until the darkness ends. There are no crossroads.**

Years later, I read a play called *The Fourth Prince Visits His Mother*. Yang Silang has been stranded in the Liao kingdom for fifteen years. He has changed his name to Mu Yi. He cannot return home. He cannot see his mother.

Onstage he sings of kneeling and bowing again and again, yet never being able to repay the guilt of a son.

I sat there—or perhaps I was only sitting inside the pages—and tears came to my eyes.

At least he knew whom he was longing for.

But me?

Whom did I long for?

My adoptive mother? I had never even seen her.

Yang Kang?

He was dead.

To long for someone who is gone is almost the same as longing for no one at all.

But the man who wrote me knew.

**He knew what it meant to long for someone. Otherwise, how could he have written it?**

Sometimes I hate him.

I hate him for making me suffer all my life.

And yet I also think that when he wrote me, perhaps he was suffering too.

---

## **Volume II · The Marriage Challenge**

The flags were flying.

The drums were beating.

The challenge platform stood high above the crowd.

My adoptive father told me:

“Stand there. Whoever defeats you will be your husband.”

Then I saw him riding toward me.

Fine clothes. A jade belt. A handsome face. A folding fan turning lazily in his hand.

He looked like a man who had never been forced to hurry for anything.

He defeated me.

He held me in his arms.

And he took my embroidered shoe.

I should have hated him.

But I didn't.

I placed all the awakening of my heart—all the tenderness I had never known what to do with—into that single glance.

Only later did I learn that his name was Wanyan Kang.

A prince of Jin.

His father was a Jurchen.

His mother was Han Chinese.

His adoptive father was his enemy.

His birth father was also, in another sense, his enemy.

He belonged completely to neither side.

And neither side could fully accept him.

But when I fell in love with him, I knew none of this.

I knew only that after that one glance, my heart no longer belonged to me.

**The man who wrote me already knew he would destroy me.**

He knew that my entire life would be ruined by that single glance.

And still he wrote it that way.

He made him steal my shoe.

He made me chase after him.

He made me walk down a road from which I would never find my way back.

Only later did I understand:

He was not merely writing a story.

**He was writing something called inevitability.**

Yang Silang could not escape the fate of captivity.

Yang Kang could not escape the fracture of his identity.

And I could not escape him.

Perhaps the man who wrote me could not escape something either.

Perhaps he knew, in his own life, what it meant to be trapped inside a fate you never chose.

So he put that feeling, grain by grain, into my bones.

---

## **Volume III · The Bowl of Porridge**

There was a time when I lived for a while near Iron Spear Temple.

Yang Kang died there.

No one collected his body.

No one buried him.

When I arrived, all that remained was a pile of bones.

I could not tell which bone had once belonged to him.

I knelt there.

I did not cry.

I simply placed a bowl of porridge on the ground.

It was the kind he used to like.

Back at Prince Zhao's residence, after drinking wine, he would sometimes come to my courtyard in the middle of the night and ask me to make him porridge.

I made bowl after bowl.

He would finish it and leave.

He never looked back.

I knew he did not love me.

He only needed somewhere to go where, for a little while, he could pretend he was not alone.

But even that bowl of porridge could no longer reach him.

It slowly grew cold.

I sat there for a long time.

When the sky began to darken, I finally stood, brushed the dust from my knees, and left.

**The man who wrote me never wrote this scene.**

He merely mentioned, almost in passing, that Mu Nianci later settled in Ox Village.

But I know he must have imagined it.

A woman kneeling on the ground at Iron Spear Temple.

A bowl of porridge going cold beside her.

He did not write it because he did not need to.

He hid the most painful things inside the silence between the sentences.

Later, I thought of how well he knew the old play *The Fourth Prince Visits His Mother*—the bird trapped in a cage, wings spread but unable to fly; the tiger driven from its mountain, left utterly alone.

Yang Silang at least had a stage on which to cry out.

I had no stage.

My throat was silent.

I swallowed every grievance I had.

**The man who wrote me must have been someone who swallowed his own grievances.**

Otherwise, how could he have known?

How could he have known that what you swallow can hurt more than anything you ever say aloud?

---

## **Volume IV · Guo'er**

I gave birth to Guo'er.

*Guo'er.*

Yang Guo.

His courtesy name was *Gaizhi*—“to correct what is wrong.”

Correct whose wrong?

Yang Kang's?

Mine?

Everyone's?

I held him in my arms.

He was so small.

So soft.

His eyes were closed.

He knew nothing.

He did not know who his father was.

He did not know what his father had done.

He did not know that his father had died at Iron Spear Temple, and that no one had even collected his bones.

He did not know that the woman who spent her life waiting for his father would never see him return.

I raised him alone.

Morning to night.

Spring to winter.

No one helped me.

No one asked whether I was tired.

When Guo'er was little, he was always running outside.

I would call him back for dinner until my throat was raw.

**The man who wrote me never wrote any of this.**

Instead, he made Guo'er into the Divine Condor Hero.

He made him wait sixteen years for Xiaolongnü.

He gave him a life of extraordinary adventures, great love, and legendary deeds.

He gave Guo'er everything he had refused to give me.

He was not playing favorites.

**He was repaying me through my son.**

Perhaps that man, too, owed something to someone.

Perhaps he had once failed someone he could not save.

So he put his debts, one by one, into the people he created on paper.

He let those characters accomplish the things he himself could not.

I do not resent him.

If anything, I am grateful.

Not because he made me suffer.

But because he gave me Guo'er.

He was the only warmth in my life.

In the story, he could wait sixteen years for the woman he loved.

When he was with me, he could not even wait fifteen minutes before running outside to play.

I waited for him all my life.

Waited for him to grow.

Waited for him to come home.

Waited for him to call me *Mother*.

He eventually became a great hero.

When I died, he did not know.

He was still wandering the world, searching for his beloved aunt.

**The man who wrote me did not arrange for my son to return for my funeral.**

Not because I was unimportant.

But because people like me are rarely given funerals.

I spent my life waiting for others.

When I died, I simply disappeared.

Perhaps that was for the best.

I did not want to trouble anyone.

---

## **Volume V · The Man Who Wrote Me**

I often wonder who he really was.

He wrote so many heroes.

So many warriors.

Guo Jing defending Xiangyang.

Qiao Feng breaking the arrow.

Yang Guo waiting sixteen years.

Every one of them was given a moment of completion.

Only I was not.

I was given four words:

**“She died in sorrow.”**

Like a dry well.

Throw a stone into it and there is no echo.

Why did he do this to me?

Later I read some essays about him.

Some said he was devoted to Chinese opera. Even after recovering from illness, he would go to see performances of *The Fourth Prince Visits His Mother*, and could recite its lyrics from memory.

Some said he borrowed many of his narrative structures from traditional opera.

Perhaps Yang Silang's predicament was rewritten inside me:

loving someone who could never return.

But why did he write me at all?

Was it merely to give Guo'er a mother?

Was I only the beginning of the story of *The Return of the Condor Heroes*?

**Or did he simply believe that people like me deserved to be remembered?**

Not because we were extraordinary.

But because we suffered, and almost no one knew.

I think he had his own *nianci* somewhere inside him.

His own longing.

His own unanswered name.

He hid it inside me.

He hid it between the pages.

When he wrote *The Legend of the Condor Heroes*, he was only in his early thirties.

Perhaps he, too, carried some attachment.

Some regret.

Some homeland he could not return to.

Some person he could no longer see.

**He wrote me because I could say, in his place, the things he himself could not say.**

But he never allowed me to cry them out.

He made me swallow them.

**Because what is swallowed is sometimes more real than what is spoken.**

What cannot be swallowed belongs to the stage.

What can be swallowed belongs to life.

---

## **Volume VI · The Lamp**

Tonight Guo'er is not at home.

The broken house in Ox Village belongs to me alone.

The wind is strong outside.

The paper on the windows rattles and flaps.

I light an oil lamp.

I place my basket of needles and thread on my knees.

I am mending a padded coat for Guo'er.

Winter is coming.

He never remembers to dress warmly.

I sew.

And sew.

And then the tears come.

Not because life is bitter.

But because I suddenly remember something.

**It seems that all my life I have been sewing clothes for other people.**

For my adoptive father.

For Yang Kang.

For Guo'er.

Countless garments.

Yet not one of them was ever made for me.

*Nianci. Nianci.*

I spent my life longing for others.

No one ever longed for me.

But that man—the man who wrote me—

**he did remember me.**

He remembered me in four words:

*She died in sorrow.*

Not because of something I accomplished.

But because of everything I never did.

He remembered my incompletion.

That is rare in books.

In real life, it is everywhere.

I put down my needle.

I walk to the window and open it a crack.

Outside, everything is black.

No moon.

No stars.

And yet something inside me suddenly becomes bright.

I understand now.

**He did not make me suffer so that I would cry.**

He made me suffer so that people who had also failed to become what they dreamed of might someday see themselves in me.

I am not a legend.

I am simply an ordinary woman.

A woman who loved the wrong man and never managed to recover from it.

There are countless women like that.

Countless lives that never become stories.

But he gave me a name.

And because of that name, I was remembered.

That is enough.

It is enough.

I close the window.

I put out the lamp.

In the darkness, I hold Guo'er's coat against my face.

The coarse cotton scratches my skin.

And suddenly I smile.

When he wrote *The Fourth Prince Visits His Mother*, Yang Silang knelt before Empress Dowager She and struck his forehead against the ground until it bled.

She touched his head and said:

“You've grown thin.”

**No one ever touched my head and told me I had grown thin.**

But the man who wrote me—

he let me live this long on paper.

Perhaps that was his own *nianci*.

His own longing.

He took the person he could never reach,

and turned her into me.

And then, somehow,

he made me long for myself.

Enough.

Dawn is coming.

I go to the kitchen.

There is some porridge left from last night.

I will warm it and leave it for Guo'er.

When he wakes, he will be hungry.

And he will eat.

—**Mu Nianci, written beneath the oil lamp in a broken house in Ox Village**
