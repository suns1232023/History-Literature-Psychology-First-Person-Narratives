# Yinglian: The Woman Abandoned by Information

### — The Final Journal of Zhen Yinglian (Xiangling)

**Keywords:** #CultureAndMedia #DreamOfTheRedChamber #InformationAsymmetry #GameTheory

**By: A Researcher of *Dream of the Red Chamber***

---

## 【Preface】

As a researcher with the *Dream of the Red Chamber* Research Association, I have spent the past two years working on a study of **asymmetric games** in *Dream of the Red Chamber*, including an analysis of the psychological mechanisms behind Jiaoxing's dramatic reversal of fortune.

After the publication of that diary, I received numerous responses from readers. One question appeared repeatedly:

**Why not write something from Yinglian's perspective?**

I was silent for a long time.

Because Yinglian's perspective is simply **too painful**.

In *Dream of the Red Chamber*, Yinglian—later known as Xiangling—is one of the characters whose sense of personal agency is most severely constrained. She rarely controls a conversation, has almost no meaningful choices, and is even deprived of a complete memory of her own past.

She is almost like a mirror: reflecting the light of others, but seemingly possessing no light of her own.

Yet a complete human being, even one trapped in the darkest corner of fate, must still possess an inner world.

This journal is my attempt to reconstruct that inner world.

Based on the first eighty chapters of the novel and the commentaries attributed to Zhiyanzhai, I reconstruct, at each critical turning point, **what Yinglian knew and what she did not know**, and examine how **incomplete information shaped the trajectory of her life**.

The language is modern Chinese rendered in plain speech. But the emotions belong to Yinglian—the girl who was abducted, forgotten, traded, and gradually consumed by the world around her.

— *A Researcher of Dream of the Red Chamber*

---

# Part I: What I Remember, and What I Don't

**Fifteenth day of the twelfth month, Jiwei Year
Heavy Snow**

The snow is falling hard today.

It looks just like that winter in the Zhen household.

My head has begun to ache again.

Whenever the pain comes, fragments rise from somewhere deep inside my mind.

A courtyard.

A tree.

A woman in red.

And a voice calling:

**“Yinglian. Yinglian.”**

But I cannot see their faces clearly.

I don't know where that courtyard was.

I don't know who that woman was.

And I don't know who “Yinglian” was.

They tell me my name is Xiangling.

Everyone in the Xue household calls me that. Xue Pan calls me Xiangling. Baochai calls me Xiangling. Even Xia Jingui, who treats me as an enemy, calls me by that name.

No one calls me Yinglian.

Perhaps Yinglian was never me.

And yet, somewhere inside, I have always felt that she was.

It is a strange feeling.

Like knowing that you are dreaming but being unable to wake.

You vaguely remember that there was someone important in the dream, something important that happened—but the moment you open your eyes, everything disappears.

The procurer who raised me said he bought me from a trafficker.

Xue Pan said he took me from Feng Yuan.

Baochai said I was her brother's concubine.

**Not one of them ever told me who I was.**

Sometimes I wonder:

**If a person does not know who she is, is she still a person?**

Perhaps not.

Perhaps I was merely an object.

Something that could be bought, sold, transferred, beaten, insulted, or given away as a possession.

Today, during another headache, I suddenly remembered an image.

An older girl wearing a green jacket was crouching down, tying my shoe.

She looked up at me and smiled.

“Miss, your shoelace has come undone again.”

She called me *Miss*.

But no one has ever called me that.

I have always been a servant.

Was that memory real?

Or did I invent it?

I don't know.

---

# Part II: Complete Information Is a Luxury

**Ninth day of the third month, Gengshen Year
Overcast**

Xue Pan hit me again today.

The reason was simple.

I spilled some tea leaves on the table while serving him.

He raised his hand and slapped me.

My ear rang.

I knelt on the floor and did not move.

I have learned one rule in the Xue household:

**Never ask why.**

Why did he hit me?

There does not need to be a reason.

Why did he curse me?

There does not need to be an explanation.

Why was I taken from one place to another?

No one owes me an answer.

In the world of the Xue family, **information flows in only one direction**.

They know.

I don't.

I do not know why Xue Pan married Xia Jingui.

I do not know why Xia Jingui hates me.

I do not know why Baochai treats me kindly.

I do not know where I will be tomorrow.

I do not know what will happen to me.

I know almost nothing.

And yet, in this world, **information is power**.

Later, I heard Baochai use a term:

**information asymmetry.**

She said that when buyers and sellers possess different amounts of information, a market can malfunction.

The seller knows that the product is defective.

The buyer does not.

The price becomes distorted.

The transaction becomes unfair.

At the time, I did not understand.

Now I do.

**My life has been a transaction conducted under radically asymmetric information.**

The procurer knew where I came from.

I did not know who he really was.

Xue Pan knew the extent of his own power.

I did not know that I was being taken from one person by force.

Jia Yucun knew who I was.

I did not know that he knew me.

There were so many things I did not know.

And precisely those things determined the course of my life.

If I had known that I was the daughter of the Zhen family, would I have resisted?

If I had known that Jia Yucun knew my father, would I have begged him for help?

If I had known what kind of man Xue Pan was, would I have chosen Feng Yuan?

But I did not know.

I knew nothing.

---

# Part III: What I Lost That Day

**Eighteenth day of the sixth month, Gengshen Year
Clear**

It is very hot today.

I am sitting in the courtyard embroidering when I suddenly remember the Lantern Festival.

I don't remember much.

Only the lights.

Red, yellow, green—everywhere.

As if the stars had fallen from the sky onto the streets.

Huo Qi was carrying me through the crowd.

I was laughing and clapping.

Then he put me down at the doorway of a household.

“Miss, wait here.”

And he walked away.

I waited for a long time.

Then a man came.

He smiled at me.

“I'm taking you to your father.”

I believed him.

Why wouldn't I?

He spoke gently.

He smiled.

He seemed kind.

Just like Huo Qi.

I don't remember what happened afterward.

I only remember that I never saw Huo Qi again.

I never saw the woman in red again.

I never returned to the courtyard with the apricot tree.

The procurer beat me.

If I cried, he beat me.

If I tried to run, he beat me.

If I mentioned my father or mother, he beat me harder.

Eventually, I stopped crying.

I stopped running.

I stopped mentioning them.

I forgot many things.

My father's face.

My mother's face.

My own name.

The procurer said that forgetting was good.

If I did not remember, I would not miss them.

If I did not miss them, I would not cry.

If I did not cry, I would not be beaten.

He was right.

I stopped crying.

But I did not understand then that someone who can no longer cry may also gradually forget how to laugh.

**That day, I did not merely lose my family.**

I lost something deeper.

**I lost the ability to know who I was.**

---

# Part IV: Feng Yuan, the Man I Was Never Supposed to Forget

**Second day of the ninth month, Gengshen Year
Rain**

It is raining today.

Softly.

As if someone is crying.

I remembered someone I had almost forgotten.

Feng Yuan.

A man I saw only once.

When the procurer sold me to him, he came to look at me.

He stood some distance away.

Then he said:

“I'll take her.”

He paid the money.

He said he would return three days later.

Those three days were the happiest days of my life.

Not because Feng Yuan was wealthy.

Not because he was handsome.

But because he looked at me differently.

The procurer looked at me as though I were a pile of silver.

Xue Pan looked at me as though I were a toy.

Xia Jingui looked at me as though I were a rat.

But Feng Yuan looked at me **as though I were a human being**.

He said he had no wife or concubine.

He said he would treat me well.

He said he did not want me merely as a servant.

He said he liked me.

He said he would give me a good life.

I believed him.

Because his eyes were clean.

But three days later, Xue Pan came.

He brought his men.

They beat Feng Yuan.

They took me.

Feng Yuan was beaten so badly that he died a few days later.

I never saw him again.

At the time, I did not even know his name.

I did not know where he lived.

I barely remembered what he looked like.

I only remembered a distant figure in blue, standing in the rain.

Later, Baochai told me his name.

“Feng Yuan.”

I sat there for a long time.

Feng Yuan.

A name that sounds like *wronged fate*.

Only then did I understand.

He had died because of me.

The only person who had treated me like a person had died trying to take me away.

But at the time, I knew nothing.

**I did not know his name.
I did not know where he was.
I did not know why he had chosen me.
I did not know that he had been beaten.
I did not know that he had died.**

I knew nothing.

**That is the price of incomplete information.**

**The things you do not know can destroy people whose lives you do not even realize you are part of.**

---

# Part V: Jia Yucun, the Man Who Knew and Did Nothing

**Eighth day of the eleventh month, Gengshen Year
Overcast**

Baochai told me something today.

She said that the magistrate who handled the case in which Xue Pan beat Feng Yuan to death and took me was Jia Yucun.

She also said that when Jia Yucun once lived at the Hulu Temple, he had received help from Zhen Shiyin.

**Zhen Shiyin was my father.**

**My family name is Zhen.**

**My name is Zhen Yinglian.**

When Baochai said these words, her voice was quiet, almost casual.

But something exploded inside my head.

Zhen Shiyin.

Hulu Temple.

The Lantern Festival.

Huo Qi.

Yinglian.

Suddenly, the fragments came together.

**I remembered.**

I remembered the courtyard.

There was an apricot tree in the back garden of the Zhen household.

In spring, it was covered with white blossoms.

When the wind blew, the petals fell like snow.

I remembered the woman in red.

She was my mother.

She called me “Lian'er.”

She held me in her arms and kissed my face.

I remembered the girl who called me *Miss*.

Her name was Jiaoxing.

She tied my shoelaces.

She gave me her sweets.

I remembered the Lantern Festival.

I remembered Huo Qi leaving me at the doorway.

I remembered the smiling stranger who came to take me away.

I remembered everything.

But what good was remembering now?

**Jia Yucun knew who I was.**

He knew my father.

He had received my father's kindness.

He knew that I was Zhen Shiyin's daughter.

He knew that I had been abducted.

He knew that I did not belong there.

**He knew everything.**

And yet he said nothing.

He ruled in favor of the Xue family.

He allowed Xue Pan to escape meaningful punishment.

Feng Yuan died.

I was taken away.

He watched.

And he did nothing.

Because he needed the protection of the great families.

Because he wanted to preserve his position.

Because he was Jia Yucun—a man who had benefited from the kindness of my father, yet turned his back on his benefactor's daughter.

I do not hate him.

I simply cannot understand.

How can someone know so much and still do nothing?

How can someone know the truth and pretend not to know?

How can knowledge become a weapon in one person's hands and a shield in another's?

Perhaps this is the cruelest feature of incomplete information.

**The person who knows can choose whether to reveal what they know.**

**The person who does not know can only be chosen.**

Jia Yucun chose silence.

So I was chosen.

---

# Part VI: Jiaoxing, the One Who Knew

**Fifteenth day of the first month, Xinyou Year
Lantern Festival**

It is the Lantern Festival again.

Xue Pan has gone drinking.

Xia Jingui has insulted me again.

I hid in the woodshed and cried while fireworks exploded outside.

Then I thought of Jiaoxing.

The girl who once tied my shoelaces.

I heard that she is now Jia Yucun's wife.

A servant girl became a magistrate's wife.

I was born a young lady and became a servant.

That was our fate.

I am not jealous of her.

Truly.

I only wonder why our lives turned out so differently.

**Jiaoxing knew something.**

She knew that Jia Yucun might one day rise.

So she noticed him.

She looked at him again.

She made herself memorable.

She knew something about the person in front of her.

And she acted on that information.

What did I know?

I knew nothing.

I did not know that the procurer was a trafficker.

I did not know what would happen that Lantern Festival.

I did not know who Feng Yuan was.

I did not know that Xue Pan would take me.

I did not know that Jia Yucun knew my father.

I did not even know who I was.

I did not know enough to act.

Not because I was lazy.

Not because I was stupid.

Not because I simply accepted my fate.

But because—

**I did not know.**

**How can someone make a meaningful choice when she does not know the game she is playing?**

**How can someone change her fate when she does not even know her own identity?**

Jiaoxing had information.

She had the ability to act upon it.

I had neither.

That is the cruel truth of an information-asymmetric world:

**You may be playing the same game as everyone else, while others know the rules, understand the incentives, recognize the players, and know when to place their bets.**

**You are not merely losing the game.**

**You were never given the information necessary to play it.**

---

# Part VII: Xia Jingui, the Woman I Could Never Understand

**Sixteenth day of the fifth month, Xinyou Year
Clear**

Xia Jingui found fault with me again today.

She said my embroidery was ugly.

She said the tea was too hot.

She said I walked too loudly.

She cursed me for an hour.

Then she made me kneel in the sun.

While kneeling, I kept asking myself:

Why does she hate me?

I have never offended her.

I never argue with her.

I barely dare to speak to her.

I simply try to remain invisible.

Yet she hates me.

Later, I understood.

**She does not hate me because of something I did.**

**She hates me because I exist.**

I am Xue Pan's concubine.

I came before her.

I can embroider.

I can write poetry.

I can speak with Baochai.

None of these things are my fault.

But in her eyes, they are.

**She needs someone to hate.**

And I happen to be there.

It made me think of Jiaoxing.

If Jiaoxing had been in the Xue household, what would she have done?

Would she have tried to win Xia Jingui over?

Would she have complained to Xue Pan?

Would she have used Jia Yucun's influence to escape?

I do not know.

Because I am not Jiaoxing.

I do not possess the information she possessed.

I do not possess her courage.

I do not possess her opportunities.

I am simply Xiangling.

**A person who does not know enough to move.**

---

# Part VIII: What I Know, and What I Don't

**Twenty-third day of the seventh month, Xinyou Year
Overcast**

Today I decided to make a list.

What do I know?

What do I not know?

### What I know:

I know that my name is Xiangling.

I know that I am Xue Pan's concubine.

I know that Xia Jingui hates me.

I know that Baochai treats me kindly.

I know that I can embroider.

I know that I can write poetry.

I know that my head often aches.

I know that I dream of a courtyard.

A tree.

A woman in red.

I know that a voice calls me “Yinglian.”

### What I do not know:

I do not know whether Yinglian is really me.

I do not know who Zhen Shiyin is.

I do not know whether the woman in red is my mother.

I do not know why Jiaoxing became a magistrate's wife.

I do not know why Jia Yucun refused to help me.

I do not know what Feng Yuan looked like.

I do not know why I am here.

I do not know what tomorrow will bring.

I do not know how long I have left to live.

**The things I do not know far outnumber the things I do.**

That is my life.

**To live inside an information-incomplete market while possessing almost no control over the information that defines one's own position.**

I was abducted.

Bought.

Sold.

Taken.

Abused.

Consumed.

Others knew.

I did not.

Others could choose.

I could not.

**That is the deepest asymmetry of all.**

It is not that I could not play the game.

**I was denied the information required to understand what game I was playing.**

---

# Part IX: The End

**Third day of the third month, Renxu Year
Clear**

The weather is beautiful today.

Sunlight falls through the window.

It feels warm.

My body is getting weaker.

Ever since giving birth, the bleeding has not stopped.

The physician came.

He shook his head.

There is nothing more to be done.

I know I am dying.

Strangely, I am not afraid.

I am simply tired.

Very tired.

For years, I have tried to answer one question:

**Who am I?**

The procurer said I belonged to him.

Xue Pan said I was the concubine he bought.

Xia Jingui called me a shameless creature.

Baochai called me Xiangling.

But I know now that I am none of those things.

**I am Yinglian.**

**I am Zhen Shiyin's daughter.**

I am the little girl who grew up in the Zhen household.

I am the child who ran beneath the apricot tree.

I am the girl Jiaoxing once called *Miss*.

I remember now.

At last.

But it is too late.

If I had remembered earlier, would things have been different?

If I had remembered my name when I was abducted, would someone have found me?

If I had known who Feng Yuan was, would I have begged him to take me away?

If I had known who Jia Yucun was when he heard my case, would I have called out for help?

If I had known these things earlier, would my life have changed?

Perhaps.

Perhaps not.

But I did not know.

**I did not know in time.**

That is the tragedy.

Information has a temporal dimension.

A fact can be true and still arrive too late to matter.

**Information has value only when it remains actionable.**

I spent more than a decade reconstructing the answer to the question of who I was.

But that answer was most valuable many years earlier.

If someone had told me then:

You are Zhen Yinglian.

You are the daughter of the Zhen family.

You were abducted.

You are not a commodity.

Would my life have been different?

Perhaps.

Perhaps not.

But at least I would have known what game I was playing.

I would have known who the players were.

I would have known what cards I held.

Instead, I was pushed onto the gambling table of fate **without even knowing the rules of the game**.

That is the ultimate tragedy of incomplete information:

**Information is not merely something we possess.**

**It has a window of usefulness.**

Once that window closes, knowledge may remain intellectually valuable while becoming practically powerless.

# Part X: My Final Letter to Jiaoxing

**Tenth day of the third month, Renxu Year
Clear**

### Dear Sister Jiaoxing,

If this letter ever reaches you, there are a few things I want to say.

**First: I still remember you.**

I remember how you tied my shoelaces.

I remember you crouching down, looking up at me, smiling:

“Miss, your shoelace has come undone again.”

I remember your voice.

Soft.

Gentle.

Like the wind in spring.

You are one of the few people I remember who treated me with kindness.

**Second: I do not blame you.**

I know you became Jia Yucun's wife.

I know you knew something about my story.

I know you did not save me.

I do not blame you.

Because perhaps you had no choice either.

In that world, everyone was constrained.

Jia Yucun was constrained.

You were constrained.

I was constrained.

We were all pushed forward by circumstances larger than ourselves.

**We were all players in a game of incomplete information.**

The difference was that you had information I did not.

And you acted upon it.

**Third: I want you to know something.**

I do not hate you.

I never did.

I only envied you.

I envied that you knew more.

I envied that you recognized an opportunity.

I envied that you made a choice.

But most of all, I envied something simpler:

**You knew who you were.**

You knew you were Jiaoxing.

You knew where you came from.

You understood something about Jia Yucun's future.

You recognized the opportunity in front of you.

And you acted.

I spent more than a decade merely trying to recover the answer to the question:

**Who am I?**

By the time I knew the answer, it was too late.

So, Sister Jiaoxing, if there is another life, I want to be like you.

I want to be someone who knows.

Someone who dares to act.

Someone who can place a bet when the opportunity appears.

Not someone who knows nothing and can only wait for others to decide her fate.

This letter may never reach you.

But I still want to write it.

Because this is the first time in my life that I am doing something entirely of my own choosing.

Consciously.

Freely.

Knowing exactly who I am.

**My name is Zhen Yinglian.**

**I am the daughter of Zhen Shiyin.**

**I was abducted.**

**I am not Xiangling.**

**I am not anyone's property.**

**I am myself.**

Goodbye, Sister Jiaoxing.

If there is another life,

I hope I can know a little more.

A little earlier.

**— Zhen Yinglian**

# Author's Afterword

After finishing this journal, I sat in silence for a long time.

Over the past two years, I have written three pieces about Jiaoxing and Yinglian.

The first was about **Jiaoxing's luck**.

The second was about **Jiaoxing's rationality**.

The third was about **Yinglian's tragedy**.

Three perspectives.

Three different trajectories.

But one underlying question:

**How does information asymmetry shape human destiny?**

Jiaoxing possessed a small piece of useful information.

She acted upon it.

Yinglian did not even know the identity that could have allowed her to defend herself.

She had no meaningful opportunity to act.

This is not simply a question of luck.

It is a question of **information, timing, agency, and power**.

In an information-incomplete environment, the person who knows more may possess an advantage.

But information alone does not determine destiny.

What matters is whether information can be obtained **in time**, and whether the person who receives it possesses the **agency and resources necessary to act upon it**.

Yinglian was not merely information-poor.

She was deprived of identity information, decision-relevant information, and ultimately the ability to convert knowledge into effective action.

She was abducted when she was only a child.

What could a five-year-old possibly understand?

She was beaten until she stopped asking questions.

She was frightened until she stopped resisting.

She was separated from the people who could have confirmed her identity.

She did not simply fail to know.

**She was prevented from knowing.**

That is a much darker form of information asymmetry.

Not merely:

> **You don't know.**

But:

> **Someone else controls whether you are allowed to know.**

And once information itself becomes controlled, information asymmetry becomes a form of power.

So what does this story mean for us?

Perhaps three things.

**First, information has a price.**

Those who do not know may pay the cost for those who do.

**Second, information has a time dimension.**

Knowledge acquired too late may have little remaining decision value.

**Third, information is power.**

Those who control information can influence the choices available to those who do not possess it.

But there is one more lesson I would add.

**Information without agency is not freedom.**

Knowing the truth matters.

But so does having the power to act upon it.

If you are one of those who knows, treat those who do not know with care.

A single sentence you casually speak may become a light in another person's life.

And if you are one of those who does not know, ask.

Investigate.

Search.

Reconstruct the fragments.

Because knowing may be the first step toward changing your circumstances.

But remember:

**The tragedy is not simply that Yinglian did not know.**

**The tragedy is that she was never given a fair opportunity to know—and by the time she finally understood who she was, the window for changing her fate had already closed.**

Do not let Yinglian's tragedy repeat itself in the real world.

**March 23, 2026**
