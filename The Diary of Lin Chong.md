# The Diary of Lin Chong

### Dongjing · The Eighth Day of the Twelfth Month, Xuanhe 2 (1120)

Today, heavy snow fell.

I was practicing spearwork in the rear courtyard of the residence when the steward came to tell me that Lu Qian had arrived.

I planted my spear upright in the snow and went out to meet him.

He was standing beneath the corridor, blowing warm breath into his hands.

He looked exactly as he always had.

In his hand was a jug of wine.

He smiled at me.

> “Brother Chong, it’s cold. Have a couple of cups.”

For some reason, I suddenly remembered when I was a boy.

I was seven that year.

Father was still alive.

He taught me how to hold a spear.

> “Chong'er, hold this spear firmly. As long as you do, no one will dare bully you.”

Father's hands were large.

They closed around my tiny fist.

Snow fell onto the shaft of the spear.

He said:

> “Snow is cold.
> The spear is cold too.
> But the strength in your hand is warm.”

I held a spear for thirty years after that.

The strength in my hand was always warm.

But the wine Lu Qian brought that night was cold.

When I drank it, I felt as though ice had filled my stomach and lungs.

He said:

> “Brother Chong, about Grand Marshal Gao… I'm afraid he has taken against you.”

I said:

> “I have always been utterly loyal to the Grand Marshal.”

He said nothing more.

He simply poured me another cup.

We drank until the third watch of the night.

When he left, he stopped at the doorway and looked back at me.

Thinking of it now, there was something in his eyes that looked like a farewell.

---

## Fifteenth Day of the Twelfth Month · White Tiger Hall

I knelt there.

Looking up at Gao Qiu.

He said:

> “Lin Chong, you entered the White Tiger Hall carrying a weapon, intending to assassinate an Imperial officer. Do you know your crime?”

The White Tiger Hall.

A forbidden place.

A center of military command.

I had served in the Imperial Guards for more than thirty years.

How could I not know?

But Grand Marshal Gao had said he wanted to inspect my treasured sword.

His men told me:

> “The Grand Marshal is waiting for you in the White Tiger Hall.”

So I brought the sword.

I entered.

And suddenly I had become a man who had illegally entered a forbidden military compound carrying a weapon.

The sword was real.

So was Gao Qiu's trap.

---

He had wanted to destroy me for a long time.

I had offended his adopted son, Gao Yanei.

I had refused to let that beast lay a hand on my wife.

I thought there was still law beneath the Son of Heaven.

I thought that I, Lin Chong, instructor of the eighty-thousand Imperial Guards, a man with rank and service behind his name, could not simply be framed by a man like Gao Qiu.

I thought.

I was wrong.

I knelt there.

Two rows of soldiers stood behind me, halberds in their hands.

Gao Qiu's face was before me.

There was nothing in his eyes.

Not hatred.

Not anger.

Not even contempt.

He simply looked at me.

As one might look at a stone.

A tree.

A dog lying in the road.

That was the day I understood:

In Gao Qiu's eyes, I was not a man.

I was a **thing**.

A thing standing in the way of his adopted son's desires.

A thing occupying a position in the Imperial Guards.

And if he wanted me removed, it was no different from moving a stone that obstructed the road.

He did not need a reason.

He only needed to say:

> “Take him.”

---

The magistrate sentenced me to exile at Cangzhou.

Twenty strokes to the back.

A tattoo across the face.

When the beating began, I did not cry out.

One stroke.

Two.

Three.

The sound of flesh splitting was dull, like a wet cloth being struck against stone.

I kept looking at the wall ahead.

Someone had carved a character into it.

I do not know who.

Blood had blurred it until it could no longer be recognized.

Suddenly I wondered:

*The person who carved that character—was he falsely accused too?*

How many people's blood had this wall received?

The pain was real.

But the pain on my body was nothing compared with the wound inside me.

---

The day I was sent to Cangzhou, my wife and her father came to see me off.

She cried so hard she could not speak.

She only held onto my sleeve.

I looked at her face.

She was the woman I had wanted to marry since I was sixteen.

I had promised her that I would never let her suffer the slightest humiliation.

I had failed.

Gao Yanei had dishonored her.

And I could not even avenge her.

I held her hand.

> “Wait for me. I will come back.”

She nodded.

Her tears fell onto the back of my hand.

My hand was warm.

Her tears were warm too.

And yet this world—

this world was cold.

---

# The Thirtieth Day of the Twelfth Month · Wild Boar Forest

Dong Chao and Xue Ba said they were going to bind me.

I held out my hands.

They pulled the rope tight until it cut into my flesh.

My bones creaked beneath it.

Then Lu Zhishen descended upon us like thunder.

One sweep of his monk's staff sent the clubs flying.

He glared at them.

> “Brother! These two bastards mean to kill you!”

I looked at Dong Chao and Xue Ba.

They were trembling like reeds in a storm.

Their mouths opened and closed, but they could barely form words.

And in that moment I thought:

I, Lin Chong, have spent my entire life serving the court.

I have served the Grand Marshal.

I have honored this uniform.

What has the court given me in return?

What has the Grand Marshal given me?

What has this uniform given me?

They want me dead.

If I die here in the Wild Boar Forest, who will remember me?

Lu Zhishen wanted to kill them.

I stopped him.

> “Brother, spare them.
> I am a man who has suffered enough.”

I said it, but inside I felt nothing.

I do not know why I pleaded for them.

Perhaps because—

I was tired.

I did not want to see another man die before my eyes.

All these years of grudges.

This life I had been carrying.

It had grown too heavy.

---

# The Sixth Month of Xuanhe 3 · Cangzhou

I arrived at the fodder depot in Cangzhou.

It was called a fodder depot, but in truth it was only a few broken sheds and an empty yard stacked with dry grass.

The old soldier assigned to guard it was leaving.

Before he went, he said:

> “Instructor Lin, life here is hard. You will have to bear with it.”

I smiled.

> “Hardship does not frighten me.”

That was true.

How much hardship had I endured in this life?

I had trained with the spear since childhood.

There were nights when my muscles and bones hurt so badly I could not sleep.

After joining the army, I slept beneath the open sky, marched through wind and rain, fought battles.

I never complained.

Then came the false accusation.

The tattoo.

The exile.

The chains.

The frost.

The rain.

The road that slowly ground me into someone else.

I was not afraid of hardship.

What frightened me was—

forgetting why I was alive.

I had to live.

I had to survive.

I had to return to Dongjing.

My wife was still there.

Waiting for me.

---

I counted the days.

One.

Two.

Three.

When the seventh day came, Fu'an arrived.

He spoke politely.

He said the Grand Marshal still remembered me.

He told me to work hard.

I looked at him.

Something inside me began to churn.

I knew him.

He was Gao Qiu's dog.

What had he come here for?

After he left, I could not sleep the entire night.

---

# The Eighth Day of the Twelfth Month, Xuanhe 3 · Mountain God Temple

Three months at the fodder depot.

That night the snow was heavy.

The shed collapsed beneath its weight.

I had no choice but to spend the night at the Mountain God Temple.

The temple was ruined.

The doorway was so low I had to stoop to enter.

I sat on the floor.

My spear rested against the wall.

That spear had been Father's.

Thirty years.

The shaft had been polished smooth by my hands.

When I held it, it felt like holding Father's hand.

Then I heard voices outside.

---

> “Lin Chong must be dead.”

Lu Qian.

I knew that voice.

> “The fire will burn everything clean.”

Fu'an.

> “When we return, tell Grand Marshal Gao that the job is done.”

The warden.

They were talking about burning me alive.

They said my wife had already been taken by Gao Yanei.

They said Lin Chong was a fool.

A joke.

Something inside my head exploded.

For a moment I could hear nothing.

All those years of endurance.

All those compromises.

All those times I had said:

> *I am a man who has suffered enough.*

All those times I had said:

> *Spare them.*

All of it—

in that instant—

turned to ash.

My hand was trembling.

I tightened my grip around the spear.

---

Lu Qian.

I had known you since I was six.

We grew up together.

We entered the Grand Marshal's household together.

We drank together.

We told each other things we told no one else.

I called you *Brother Qian*.

You called me *Brother Chong*.

I thought we would be brothers for life.

And now you had come to burn me alive.

You were sitting on a stone outside the temple, talking with Fu'an about the reward.

Talking about how you would return and report your success to Gao Qiu.

You were sitting so close to me.

There was only one broken wooden door between us.

And through that door, your mouth was speaking my death.

---

I wanted to burst out.

But I did not.

I needed to listen.

I needed to hear every word.

I needed to remember every one of their faces.

Because those words—

I, Lin Chong,

would never forget them.

Lu Qian said:

> “His wife… Gao Yanei has already…”

I did not hear the rest.

I stood.

I pushed open the door.

The snowlight stabbed my eyes.

I saw the three faces.

Lu Qian's face was pale beneath the snow.

Almost blue.

The instant he saw me, his pupils contracted.

His mouth moved.

He wanted to say something.

I did not let him.

---

I charged.

My spear pierced Fu'an.

Only then did I realize how fast my spearwork had become.

Thirty years of practice had not been wasted.

Lu Qian turned and tried to run.

I shouted:

> “Lu Qian! Stop!”

He stopped.

He turned.

He looked at me.

I saw him trembling.

The man who had grown up beside me.

The man who had drunk with me until midnight.

He was afraid of me.

He said:

> “Brother Chong…”

I said:

> “Don't call me that.”

I walked toward him.

The spearpoint touched his chest.

I could feel his heartbeat through the cloth.

Fast.

Like a drum.

I asked:

> “Why?”

He said:

> “Gao Qiu… I had no choice…”

I laughed.

> “No choice?”

I looked at him.

> “You have known me since I was six years old. And you tell me you had no choice?”

He lowered his head.

He said nothing.

I looked at his face.

And suddenly I remembered that winter when we were drinking.

He was drunk.

He held my hand and said:

> “Brother Chong, you are the only friend I have in this life.”

I tightened my grip around the spear.

The instant I drove it into his chest—

**my hand was still warm.**

---

# The Twentieth Day of the Twelfth Month, Xuanhe 3 · Liangshan Marsh

I came to Liangshan.

I sat in the gathering hall and looked at the faces around me.

Some were familiar.

Some were not.

They called me:

*Instructor Lin.*

*Brother.*

*Leopard-Head.*

I smiled at them.

I drank with them.

But I always felt that something inside me was missing.

A piece had been cut away.

---

Sometimes I wake in the middle of the night.

I stare out the window.

I think of Dongjing.

Of that small courtyard.

Of the jujube tree standing there.

Perhaps my wife is still waiting for me.

I do not know.

I think of Lu Qian.

I remember the final glimmer in his eyes when my spear entered his chest.

It seemed he wanted to say something.

I heard nothing.

---

Father once said:

> “Chong'er, hold this spear firmly.
> As long as you do, no one will dare bully you.”

Now no one dares bully me.

But I am no longer the man I was.

I no longer believe in the law.

I no longer believe in justice.

I no longer believe that the Grand Marshal will “see the truth.”

I no longer believe that if a man endures for one moment, the storm will pass.

I no longer believe that stepping back will reveal a wider road.

I believe in this spear.

I believe in the hand that holds it.

I believe there is only one thing in this world that is real:

**the strength in your own hand.**

**The fire that burns inside you.**

Lu Qian was right.

I was a fool.

But this fool has finally learned what it means to be alive.

---

Snow is still falling.

The winter at Liangshan is as cold as that night at the Mountain God Temple.

I run my hand along the shaft of my spear.

It is still warm.
