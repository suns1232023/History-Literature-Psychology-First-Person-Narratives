# The Game of Iron

### — The Private Confession of Qiu Qianren

**Iron Palm Peak — Secret Chamber**

The candle flame trembles inside its bronze cup, casting my shadow across the stone wall.

Huge.

Shifting.

Like a beast trapped in a cage.

Outside the window, a gibbon cries.

Once.

Then again.

Each cry seems to tear another opening in the night.

Today, I did something.

Something as small as a mustard seed—

yet powerful enough to shift the mountains of the martial world.

I struck an infant.

Only a few months old.

Barely two feet long.

No more than ten pounds.

Soft as a handful of newly fallen snow.

When my Iron Palm landed, blood spattered across the back of my hand.

Warm.

With the faint, milky smell of infancy.

That warmth suddenly reminded me of many years ago, when I first drove a blade into a man's body.

When I pulled it out, blood sprayed across my face.

It was warm then, too.

And rank.

But that time my hand had trembled.

Today, it did not.

I thought I had long since become incapable of feeling anything about killing.

The people who have died by my hand, if their bodies were piled one atop another, might stand as high as Iron Palm Peak itself.

In my younger days, I lived by the blade, fighting for enough food to survive.

Later, I founded my own sect.

One night, I slaughtered the entire Hengshan household.

From the white-haired master to the children barely old enough to tie their hair—

I spared no one.

Afterward, I slept for three days and three nights.

Not from fear.

From satisfaction.

At last, the martial world remembered the name **Qiu Qianren**.

Yet today, that tiny warmth in my palm felt like a branding iron.

It burned somewhere deep inside me.

That child was too small.

So small that one of my hands could cover his entire face.

When he looked at me, his eyes were cloudy and unfocused, like those of a newborn fawn.

There was no fear in them.

Only innocent curiosity.

Perhaps he thought I was playing with him.

Then my palm struck his back.

He did not even cry.

His little body simply convulsed.

Blood burst from his mouth.

His rosy face turned blue-purple in an instant, like a flower withering before my eyes.

When Yinggu rushed toward him, her eyes were those of a poisoned she-wolf.

I watched her fall.

Watch her struggle to rise.

Fall again.

Then crawl forward, her fingernails digging into the cracks between the bricks as she reached for her child.

Something inside me made a sound.

A tiny—

**click.**

So faint that I almost missed it.

But I heard it.

---

## I. The Game

I, Qiu Qianren, never act without calculation.

Every step is a move.

Every piece must be calculated ten moves ahead.

Over the years, the Iron Palm Sect has grown stronger and stronger.

Yet five mountains still stand above me.

The Eastern Heretic.

The Western Venom.

The Southern Emperor.

The Northern Beggar.

And the Central Divinity, though dead, has left a place so firmly cast in iron that the title itself remains.

Whenever the martial world speaks of the greatest masters, people still speak of the **Five Greats**.

And what am I?

**The greatest beneath the Five Greats.**

What a phrase.

*Beneath.*

Who remembers the name of the second-highest mountain in the world?

People look only toward Everest.

I refuse to accept that.

Hong Qigong's Eighteen Dragon-Subduing Palms are devastatingly powerful, but too straightforward.

Ouyang Feng's Toad Style is strange and dangerous, yet too unconventional.

Huang Yaoshi knows an extraordinary breadth of arts, but never truly walks the orthodox path.

Wang Chongyang is dead.

Only Duan Zhixing—the ruler of Dali—remains truly difficult to shake.

His **Solitary Yang Finger** is unrivaled, and he possesses the natural foundation of an emperor.

But he has one obvious weakness.

He cares too much about dignity.

A former emperor who has entered the Buddhist path still carries power and pride inside his heart.

That is the rib beneath which I can strike.

I need a victory.

Not an ordinary victory.

A victory powerful enough to shake the martial world—

powerful enough to pry one corner from the signboard of the Five Greats and replace it with the name **Qiu Qianren**.

A direct challenge?

Too risky.

If we both suffer, someone else merely profits from our struggle.

No.

I need to make him fight me.

And I need him to fight with chains around his ankles.

That child is the chain.

Yinggu's child, born in the emperor's secluded palace, has long been the subject of whispered rumors throughout the martial world.

Duan Zhixing's humiliation is known everywhere.

He hates.

Yet he must pretend magnanimity.

He suffers.

Yet he must preach compassion.

If I injure the child, only his **Solitary Yang Finger** can save him.

If Duan Zhixing saves the child, he will expend five years of his cultivation.

Then I strike.

My chance of victory: seventy percent.

If he refuses to save the child, the reputation of the Southern Emperor and revered Buddhist master will be stained by the charge of standing by while an innocent child dies.

Either way,

**I win.**

Three gains from a single move:

**The superior strategy:**
He saves the child. His power is diminished. I challenge him and defeat him. I enter the ranks of the Five Greats.

**The middle strategy:**
He refuses. His reputation collapses. The Iron Palm Sect expands southward into Yunnan.

**The inferior strategy:**
Yinggu's hatred burns into something permanent.

And a woman's hatred can be the sharpest blade of all.

So today I went to Dali.

I found the secluded palace.

I found the cradle.

Everything went according to calculation.

Except for one thing.

**My old injury returned.**

---

## II. The Injury

Three months ago, beside Dongting Lake, I exchanged a palm with Hong Qigong.

It was not a formal challenge.

We simply crossed paths.

That old beggar was dressed in rags, yet there was something terrifyingly bright in his eyes.

Our words went nowhere.

His palm came anyway.

His Eighteen Dragon-Subduing Palms truly deserve their reputation as the most powerful force under heaven.

I took one blow directly against my left ribs.

Two ribs broke.

My internal energy has been in disorder ever since.

Today, when I struck the child, the moment I summoned my internal force, my chest split with pain like torn silk.

That palm contained no more than half my strength.

I had originally intended to leave the child with perhaps one day of life, forcing Duan Zhixing to make his choice immediately.

Now he may have another day or two.

It makes little difference.

The game remains the same.

Death comes sooner or later.

What troubles me is not the child's injury.

It is my own.

If Duan Zhixing truly accepts my challenge, the condition of my body introduces another variable.

I will need to enter seclusion and force Hong Qigong's remaining palm force out of my meridians.

But another voice has appeared in my heart.

*Qiu Qianren.*

*When did you sink so low that you must raise your hand against an infant?*

I pushed the voice back down.

The martial world is a millstone made of flesh and blood.

Those who hesitate are ground into dust.

Thousands of people in the Iron Palm Sect depend upon me for their livelihood.

They depend upon me for their strength.

I cannot retreat.

One step backward,

and beneath me lies an abyss.

---

## III. Those Eyes

Yinggu's eyes made me uncomfortable.

Not fear.

I have killed countless people.

Many fear me.

I have long since become numb to it.

Her eyes held hatred at their surface.

But beneath the hatred was something else.

A fire burning beneath ice.

As though she intended to carve my face permanently into her pupils,

carry it with her into the grave,

and recognize me again in another life.

I am not afraid of revenge.

She is only a fragile woman.

Even if she trained for the rest of her life, she would scarcely possess one-tenth of my strength.

But that look was like a tiny splinter lodged between my fingers.

It does not hurt if I leave it alone.

Touch it,

and it irritates.

Perhaps my internal injury has unsettled my mind.

Perhaps I am imagining things.

---

## IV. How Will Duan Zhixing Choose?

After leaving Dali, I spent the night at an inn.

I lay on the bed and tried to crawl inside Duan Zhixing's skin.

To think as he would think.

**He is a king.**

A king values dignity above almost everything.

A woman's betrayal.

An illegitimate child.

Humiliation already known across the world.

And now, if he spends years of cultivation to save the child, it is as though he is announcing to all under heaven:

*I can be humiliated.*

The dignity of an emperor cannot withstand that.

**He studies Buddhism.**

Buddhism teaches compassion.

Yet to watch a dying child and do nothing contradicts everything the scriptures teach.

Late at night, alone before a single lamp, will his conscience gnaw at him?

I do not know.

Nor do I care.

But if he fears that the reputation of the revered Buddhist master will be damaged, he may grit his teeth and save the child.

**He knows me.**

With his intelligence, he will surely see that this is a trap.

Once he knows the bait is poisoned, will he swallow it?

Or walk away?

If he refuses to give me what I want, even at the cost of his reputation, then he will not save the child.

So I calculate:

**Seventy percent he refuses.
Thirty percent he saves.**

Enough.

Whatever he chooses, I have another move waiting.

If he saves the child—

his power is greatly diminished.

I send him a challenge.

A fair contest.

If I win, my name becomes legend.

If I lose, I can blame my old injury.

The martial world always leaves room for excuses.

If he refuses—

the child dies.

The mother becomes my enemy.

Dali's reputation collapses.

The Iron Palm Sect expands southward.

Then I spread another rumor:

*Duan, the Southern Emperor, wears the face of Buddha but carries a heart of stone.*

*He is unworthy of the title.*

Sooner or later,

that throne will have to be given up.

However I look at it,

**this is my game to win.**

---

## V. But What Am I Afraid Of?

The deeper the night becomes,

the more mournful the gibbon's cries grow.

Like an infant crying in the dark.

Whenever I close my eyes, I see that little blue-purple face.

Those cloudy eyes.

I have killed countless people.

I never think about them.

What is there to remember about the dead?

Only bodies.

Yet this child's face refuses to leave me.

Because he was too small?

Because he could not fight back?

Or because—

somewhere inside me, I know what I did was beneath me?

*Beneath me?*

Ha.

The martial world judges victory and defeat.

It does not judge honor.

I, Qiu Qianren, did not reach this position through benevolence and righteousness.

Yet today, before victory or defeat has even been decided,

I already feel disgusted with myself.

This is not an injury of the body.

**It is an illness of the heart.**

I remember the first man I killed as a young man.

He mocked the poverty of my master and me.

I exploded.

I drove a knife into his stomach.

When he fell, his eyes were wide open.

As though asking:

*Was this really worth killing for?*

That night I dreamed of blood-covered men chasing me for a hundred miles.

Later, I killed more people.

Then more.

The dead began lining up in my dreams.

There were so many that, eventually, there was no room left for the next one.

But this child's face has cut the line.

Has the old man grown weak?

Have I become soft?

No.

Softness means death.

How many people in the martial world are waiting for me to fall?

How many enemies of the Iron Palm Sect are waiting for a weakness?

If I show fear for even one moment, someone will step onto my corpse tomorrow and take my place.

I must be hard.

That child was merely a piece on the board.

No different from a sword.

A chess piece does not know pain.

A sword does not cry.

When he dies,

he will know nothing.

How many infants die in this world every day?

War.

Famine.

Pestilence.

Do they not kill as well?

One more death makes no difference.

I tell myself this.

Again.

And again.

Yet I still cannot sleep.

---

## VI. Tomorrow

Tomorrow I must return to Dali.

I need to see whether the child is dead.

Whether Duan Zhixing has acted.

And if the child is still alive—

should I strike him again?

No.

Too dangerous.

Sneaking into the palace was already a reckless move.

I cannot risk leaving another trace.

The best outcome is that Duan Zhixing refuses to save him.

The child dies.

Cleanly.

My hand merely injured him.

The killing belongs to Duan Zhixing.

If the world condemns someone, let it condemn him for standing by while the child died.

What does that have to do with Qiu Qianren?

Yes.

That is best.

He carries the disgrace.

I take the advantage.

At the thought, my heart relaxes slightly.

The child's face becomes blurred.

It fades into the ordinary face of an infant.

All infants look alike.

There is nothing special about this one.

I turn over and prepare to sleep.

Just before closing my eyes, I glance at my right hand.

My Iron Palm.

There seems to be a dark red stain caught in the lines of my palm.

I rub it.

It does not disappear.

Blood?

Or is the night simply too deep,

and my eyes deceiving me?

Enough.

Tomorrow's troubles belong to tomorrow.

---

## VII. Who Am I?

I have always considered myself a clever man.

I know what I want.

I know how to obtain it.

I want power.

Status.

I want the martial world to hear my name and tremble.

Love is unnecessary.

Fear is enough.

Yet sometimes, on nights like this, I ask myself:

**Qiu Qianren, who are you?**

You are the master of the Iron Palm Sect.

A warlord of the martial world.

A demon everyone fears.

But this “I”—

did I choose to become this man?

Or did the world force me into becoming him?

I was poor when I was young.

People looked down upon me.

I wanted to become strong.

Strong enough that no one would ever dare bully me again.

And eventually,

I truly became strong.

But then I could no longer stop.

Like a boulder rolling down a mountain.

The farther it falls,

the faster it goes.

The heavier it becomes.

Until it can no longer stop.

Until it can no longer turn back.

When I was young, I probably would have disdained something like striking an infant.

Not because I was kind.

Because I would have considered it beneath me.

Back then, I believed a true hero should win openly.

If you win,

you should win beautifully.

When did that change?

Perhaps when I discovered that honor and openness cannot buy power.

Hong Qigong is honorable.

Yet he remains merely the leader of the Beggars' Sect.

Ouyang Feng is ruthless.

Yet he commands the Western Regions.

Duan Zhixing was born into imperial blood.

He never needed to dirty his hands.

And what did I, Qiu Qianren, have?

Only these Iron Palms.

And a heart willing to risk everything.

Perhaps one day,

if I finally stand at the summit,

and look back upon the road I have traveled,

all I will see is a path paved with bones.

The bones of enemies.

The bones of innocents.

And perhaps,

my own.

Will I feel satisfied then?

I do not know.

But I know this:

**I cannot stop.**

To stop is to die.

So let me keep walking.

What does it matter if I struck an infant?

The victor becomes king.

The defeated become outcasts.

It has always been so.

**Qiu Qianren**
*Written in the secret chamber of Iron Palm Peak, deep into the final watch of the night.*
