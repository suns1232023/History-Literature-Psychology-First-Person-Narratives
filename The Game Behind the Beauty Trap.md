# The Game Behind the Beauty Trap

## — A Tool's Neurobiological Journey Through the Mind

### 【Third Year of Jian'an · After the Fall of Xiapi】

---

## March 21 — Overcast

He is dead.

I heard that sigh from White Gate Tower.

Through layer upon layer of courtyard walls, through the countless footsteps of armored soldiers, I heard it.

It was not the lament of a hero at the end of his road.

> **It was the final groan of a tool before it was discarded.**

Everyone says that I was the protagonist of the beauty trap, the beautiful woman whose charms brought ruin to men and kingdoms.

But no one has ever asked me:

> **As a weapon, how was I different from Lü Bu as a weapon?**

He killed people openly.

I killed people with a smile.

Today, I want to record this life in my own words.

**Not to defend myself, but so that another "me," a thousand years from now, might see this:**

> **The fate of a tool has never been in the tool's own hands.**

---

## March 22 — Rain

My name is Diao Chan.

That name was given to me by Wang Yun.

Before that, I was merely an ordinary singing girl in the Minister's residence. I had almost forgotten my own birth name.

When Wang Yun took me in, I was still very young.

He said I was beautiful.

He taught me singing and dancing, the qin and chess, how to smile, how to read people's eyes, and how to make a man unable to look away.

I thought he was raising me.

Only later did I realize:

> **He was sharpening a weapon.**

Just as Ding Yuan sharpened Lü Bu.

Just as Dong Zhuo sharpened Lü Bu.

Just as Cao Cao would later sharpen Lü Bu.

**We were all weapons.**

The only difference was that he was made of iron, while I was made of jade.

Iron could cut people.

Jade could seduce them.

But fundamentally, we were the same.

In the eyes of our masters, there were only two categories:

> **Useful and useless.**

The first time I saw Lü Bu was in the rear hall of the Minister's residence.

He arrived riding the Red Hare.

The sound of its hooves made the floor tiles tremble.

I secretly watched him from behind the curtain.

What a formidable general.

Eight chi tall, handsome features, armor flashing coldly in the sunlight.

His gaze was like an eagle's, as though he were constantly evaluating:

*Can this person be killed?*

*Is this person useful?*

*Is this person worth following?*

At that moment, I thought:

> **There must be a string inside his brain, stretched impossibly tight.**

Later, I heard that it was called the **amygdala** — the part of the brain associated with fear.

Perhaps Lü Bu's amygdala was twice the size of an ordinary man's.

That would explain why he spent his entire life running.

Always searching for somewhere safer.

But he did not understand one thing:

> **There is no safe place in this world.**

There are only more dangerous traps.

---

## March 23 — Windy

The day Wang Yun arranged for Lü Bu and me to meet, I wore my most beautiful clothes and sang my finest song.

Lü Bu's eyes remained fixed on me, like a wolf watching its prey.

But I knew:

> **The person truly being watched was me.**

Wang Yun watched from behind the scenes.

His gaze was different from Lü Bu's.

Lü Bu looked at me because he wanted me.

**Wang Yun looked at me because he was calculating me.**

He was calculating:

> How much can this piece be worth once it is placed on the board?

That night, after Lü Bu had left, Wang Yun took my hand and said:

> "Chan'er, you did very well. Once Dong Zhuo is dead, you will be a hero. I will take you as my adopted daughter, and you will live in glory for the rest of your life."

I knelt and thanked him.

Tears gathered in my eyes.

Not because I was moved.

Because I knew:

> **He was lying to me.**

The physician from the Western Regions once told me that this was called **"oxytocin deception."**

The human brain releases oxytocin to establish trust and social attachment.

But when the other person is merely acting, your oxytocin system can be hijacked.

You think someone genuinely cares about you.

In reality, they are simply exploiting your instinct to attach.

**Wang Yun was not the first person to exploit me.**

**Nor would he be the last.**

**Lü Bu was not the last either.**

They were all tools.

I was a tool too.

Tools and tools cannot have genuine feelings for one another.

There is only exploitation —

> **and deeper exploitation.**

---

## March 24 — Clear

The most brilliant part of the chain scheme was the period when I moved between Dong Zhuo and Lü Bu.

That old criminal Dong Zhuo looked at me as though he wanted to swallow me whole.

When he took me from Wang Yun's residence to the Chancellor's mansion, he waved his hand and rewarded me with piles of gold and jewels.

I smiled and thanked him.

But inside I was thinking:

> **Do you know that the head sitting on your shoulders is worth an entire kingdom?**

Every time Lü Bu came to see me, jealousy burned in his eyes.

He knew what Dong Zhuo was doing to me, but he dared not act.

He was Dong Zhuo's adopted son.

He was Dong Zhuo's weapon.

> **A weapon cannot ask its master for the master's woman.**

One evening, Lü Bu secretly slipped away to the Phoenix Pavilion to meet me.

I cried to him:

> "I was yours, yet the Grand Tutor has taken me away. In life I belong to the Lü family; in death I will be a ghost of the Lü family."

He held me in his arms.

His whole body was trembling.

I could feel his heartbeat.

It was pounding like a war drum.

For a moment, I almost softened.

But then I remembered Wang Yun waiting behind the scenes.

I remembered the fate that had been assigned to me.

And I forced my tears back.

The physician from the Western Regions told me that this was called:

> **"Prefrontal cortical inhibition of the limbic system."**

In simple terms, reason overcomes emotion.

I knew exactly what I was doing.

> **I was using a woman's tears to purchase a man's life.**

Later, Lü Bu really did kill Dong Zhuo.

When he came to me carrying Dong Zhuo's severed head, his body was covered in blood and his eyes were filled with triumph.

He said:

> "Chan'er, I killed Dong Zhuo for you. You are mine now."

I threw myself into his arms and wept uncontrollably.

But inside, I was thinking:

> **You are wrong.**

> **You did not kill him for me. You killed him for yourself.**

Your amygdala told you that Dong Zhuo's killing threshold was only two inches away.

If you did not kill him, he would kill you.

I was merely the excuse you gave yourself.

> **We are all excuses.**

> **You are Wang Yun's excuse.**

> **I am yours.**

---

## March 25 — Overcast

After Dong Zhuo's death, Lü Bu was appointed General Fenwu.

I became his concubine.

I thought life would finally improve.

But it did not take long before Li Jue and Guo Si returned.

Lü Bu fled with me.

From Chang'an to Luoyang.

From Luoyang to Xuzhou.

Along the way, he changed masters again and again:

Yuan Shu.

Yuan Shao.

Zhang Yang.

Liu Bei.

Every time we arrived somewhere new, he would say:

> "This time we're safe. This backer is powerful enough."

But we were never safe.

I began to understand.

> **He was not looking for a powerful patron.**

> **He was running for his life.**

There was an alarm inside his brain that never stopped ringing.

The physician called it the **HPA axis**.

At the slightest sign of danger, cortisol surged.

And he had to run.

I followed him for three years.

For three years, I watched him change from a magnificent general into a paranoid man who trusted no one.

He began to suspect everyone.

Chen Gong.

Gao Shun.

Zhang Liao.

Even me.

One night, he suddenly grabbed my wrist.

He asked:

> "Are you deceiving me too? Were you sent by Wang Yun to destroy me?"

His grip hurt so badly that tears came to my eyes.

Yet I smiled and shook my head.

> "General, I belong to you."

He released my wrist.

Then he held me and cried.

**A man who could kill Ding Yuan and Dong Zhuo was holding a woman and crying.**

At that moment, I realized:

He was not a hero.

> **He was a child being devoured by fear.**

But how was I any different?

I was a child devoured by fear too.

I feared Wang Yun.

I feared Dong Zhuo.

I feared Lü Bu.

I feared this chaotic age.

I used my beauty and tears as weapons.

But:

> **A weapon has no place to call home.**

---

## March 26 — Cloudy

During the days when Xiapi was under siege, Lü Bu drank on the city wall every day.

He refused to listen to Chen Gong.

He no longer trusted Gao Shun's loyalty.

He trusted only his instincts.

But his instincts had already failed him.

**Years of dopaminergic overload had impaired the normal judgment of his prefrontal cortex.**

He believed he could still mount the Red Hare and fight his way through the enemy lines as he had once done.

But he had forgotten one thing:

> **He was no longer the Lü Bu he had once been.**

His men began whispering among themselves:

> "The lord is looking for another powerful patron again. This time he wants Cao Cao. But he's changed patrons so many times. When has it ever ended well?"

Hou Cheng.

Song Xian.

Wei Xu.

They bound Lü Bu and surrendered to Cao Cao.

When I heard the news, I did not cry.

Because I knew this day would come eventually.

> **A man who constantly betrays others will eventually be betrayed by everyone.**

This was his killing line.

And his fate.

At White Gate Tower, Lü Bu was bound.

Cao Cao asked whether he would surrender.

Liu Bei said:

> "My lord, have you forgotten how Lü Bu treated Ding Jianyang and the Grand Tutor Dong?"

With that single sentence, Lü Bu's fate was sealed.

I watched from a distance.

I did not plead for him.

Not because I was cruel.

Because I knew:

> **In Cao Cao's eyes, Lü Bu and I were the same — both weapons.**

A weapon has no right to plead for another weapon.

---

## March 27 — Overcast Turning to Clear

Lü Bu is dead.

Cao Cao did not kill me.

He sent me to Guan Yu.

Later, Guan Yu did not want me either, and I was sent back.

**I was passed from man to man like a piece of merchandise.**

No one asked whether I wanted it.

No one asked what my name was.

They cared only about my face.

My body.

Whether I could make them happy.

The physician from the Western Regions said:

> **This is called "objectification."**

When a person is deprived of agency, the brain can enter a state of emotional numbness.

Dopamine is no longer released in response to pleasure.

Oxytocin no longer produces trust.

The amygdala no longer screams in response to fear.

> **You become a walking corpse.**

Perhaps that is what I became.

Yet I did not die.

I am still alive.

Because I am waiting for someone.

Not a man.

A woman like me.

She will appear a thousand years from now.

She too will be treated as a tool.

She too will be exploited by men.

And in the dead of night, she too will ask herself:

> **Who am I, really?**

I want to tell her:

> **You are not a tool.**

> **You are yourself.**

---
